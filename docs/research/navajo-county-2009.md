# Navajo County, Arizona: Local Power Actors as of October 2009

Research for a strategy game set in October 2009. Format: **Name**: role (source URL).
- **Verified** means a source dated 2008–2010, or a later source that gives the person's tenure dates, places them in the role in Oct 2009.
- **UNVERIFIED** means it comes from general knowledge, from a source outside the 2009 window, or from inference. Check these before relying on them.
- Research date: Nov 2026. Sources are mainly web search results (official PDFs, Navajo-Hopi Observer [nhonews.com], Navajo Times, KNAU, ICT, Painted Desert Tribune archives, Wikipedia, SEC filings).

## 0. Big picture for October 2009 (key game hooks)

- **The Navajo Nation had a constitutional crisis in this exact month.** On **Oct 26, 2009** the Navajo Nation Council voted 48–22 to put President Joe Shirley Jr. on paid administrative leave over the OnSat/BCDS contract scandals. VP Ben Shelly became acting president. A tribal court reinstated Shirley on Dec 14, 2009, the evening before the Dec 15 referendum on cutting the Council from 88 to 24 seats. (https://www.nhonews.com/news/president-shirley-placed-on-leave-by-council/article_2ba13888-bb6d-5369-aa69-0c89adfa3083.html ; https://navajotimes.com/news/2009/1209/121509shirleyback.php ; https://indianz.com/News/2009/017155.asp)
- **The Hopi Tribe had no Chairman or Vice Chairman all of 2009.** Ben Nuvamsa and VP Todd Honyaoma Sr. resigned effective Dec 31, 2008. An interim council government contested as illegal ran the tribe until Leroy Shingoitewa was elected Nov 18, 2009 and sworn in Dec 1, 2009. (https://www.knau.org/local-headlines/2009-11-19/hopi-elect-new-chairman-and-vice-chair ; https://www.nhonews.com/features/hopi-chairman-vice-chairman-candidates-set/article_07fb8f83-0163-599f-a9d6-8f0ad7b00130.html)
- **The H1N1 pandemic's fall wave was under way.** Mass vaccination ran across the Navajo Nation on Oct 7, 2009. CDC found American Indian/Alaska Native H1N1 mortality about 4× other groups. (https://h1n1.navajo-nsn.gov/ ; https://www.cdc.gov/Mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5848a1.htm)
- **The recession hit hard.** Navajo County unemployment was **15.7% in Oct 2009** and the 2009 average was 15.0%. Arizona's 2009 average was about 9–10%. (https://oeo.az.gov/sites/default/files/data/laus/laus-nsa-04cnt-2000to2009.pdf ; current revised BLS series)

## 3. Tribal Governments

### Navajo Nation (capital: Window Rock, Apache County)
- **Joe Shirley Jr.**: President, Jan 2003 to Jan 2011, and term-limited. **Placed on paid administrative leave by the Council Oct 26, 2009; reinstated by Window Rock District Judge Geraldine Benally Dec 14, 2009.** Pushed the initiatives to cut the Council from 88 to 24 seats and to gain a line-item veto (referendum Dec 15, 2009). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Shirley_Jr. ; https://navajotimes.com/politics/2009/1009/102709allegations.php ; https://navajotimes.com/news/2009/1209/121509shirleyback.php
- **Ben Shelly**: Vice President from Jan 2007. **Acting President from Oct 26, 2009.** A Council motion to suspend him as well failed. He was elected President in 2010. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shelly ; https://indianz.com/News/2009/017087.asp
- **Lawrence T. Morgan** (Iyanbito/Pinedale): Speaker of the 21st Navajo Nation Council, 2003–2011, re-elected Jan 2009 to a fourth term. He was the main rival of Shirley. Council spokesman: **Joshua Butler**. Shirley spokesman: **George Hardeen**. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_T._Morgan
- **Navajo Nation Council**: 88 delegates (21st Council, 2007–2011). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_Nation_Council
- **Samson Cowboy**: Executive Director, Navajo Division of Public Safety (2009). He testified to the Senate in July 2009 on 225 gangs on the reservation. https://navajotimes.com/news/2009/0809/080509gangs.php
- **Navajo Police chief**: **vacant** from April 2008, when Chief Jim Benally was put on leave, until 2016. **Bobby Etsitty** was Acting Tribal Police Chief in Nov 2009. Districts include **Kayenta** and **Dilkon** in Navajo County. https://indianz.com/News/2013/05/20/navajo-nation-without-chief-of.asp ; https://navajotimes.com/news/2009/1109/112509deputies.php
- **Anslem Roanhorse Jr.**: Executive Director, Navajo Division of Health (2009). The Division ran an Incident Command Center for H1N1 in Window Rock and a Bio-Terrorism Preparedness Program (St. Michaels). https://www.nhonews.com/features/navajo-health-director-receives-impact-award/article_50c561ab-83d0-5f05-b45d-c9d4a07f26e6.html ; https://h1n1.navajo-nsn.gov/

## 5. Major employers and infrastructure

- **Cholla Power Plant** (APS, near Joseph City): 4 coal units totalling **1,027 MW net**. Units 1–3 are owned by APS; Unit 4 (the largest, ~395–414 MW) is owned by **PacifiCorp** and operated by APS. Cooling comes from Cholla Lake on the Little Colorado River. Coal came from the McKinley Mine (NM) until it closed in 2009, then from Lee Ranch / El Segundo. About 200 workers (2020 figure). https://www3.epa.gov/epawaste/coal/pdf/aps_cholla_final.pdf ; https://www.aps.com/-/media/APS/APSCOM-PDFs/Utility/CCR-Documents/Cholla/Facility-Wide/CH_GW_AnCAR_020_20240131.pdf?sc_lang=en ; https://www.azcentral.com/story/money/business/energy/2020/01/07/pacificorp-close-generator-cholla-power-plant-northern-arizona/2829342001/
- **Coronado Generating Station** (SRP, about 6 mi NE of St. Johns, **Apache County**): 2 coal units of about 380 MW each (~773 MW total), commissioned 1979–80. https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/projects/coronado-generating-station-st-johns-arizona/
- **Catalyst Paper, Snowflake mill** (recycled newsprint, 347,000 t/yr capacity): **operating but in heavy curtailment in 2009.** About 100 laid off by Jan 2009. In July 2009 it was idled about 20 days with nearly 300 employees affected, which Catalyst blamed on the US "black liquor" tax credit for kraft mills. GM was **John McKee** (Jan 2009). It owned **The Apache Railway Co.** Closed permanently Sept 30, 2012. Catalyst was also a party in the Little Colorado River water adjudication. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144906/000114490609000028/exhibit99-1.htm ; https://www.knau.org/knau-and-arizona-news/2009-01-23/snowflake-paper-mill-cuts-back-again ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144906/000127956914000351/v370743_ex99-1.htm
- **BNSF Southern Transcon**: runs through Winslow, Joseph City and Holbrook. Amtrak's Southwest Chief stops at Winslow. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winslow,_Arizona. Details on Winslow's crew-change and yard role: **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Winslow–Lindbergh Regional Airport** (INW): built 1929 by TAT / Lindbergh. No airline service, and the USFS has an air-tanker base there. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winslow%E2%80%93Lindbergh_Regional_Airport
- **Show Low Regional Airport** (SOW): whether it had scheduled airline service in 2009, and with which carrier, is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Northland Pioneer College** (Navajo County Community College District; administration in Holbrook):

## 8. Tensions and realities useful for gameplay

1. **Navajo–Hopi land dispute and the Bennett Freeze.** The 1966 BIA development freeze covered about 1.5 million acres of western Navajo land around Tuba City. The tribes settled by compact on Nov 3, 2006, and **President Obama signed the repeal (S. 39, Public Law 111-18) on May 8, 2009**. It was Rep. Kirkpatrick's first bill signed into law (with McCain). Rehabilitation funding fights followed. The older 1974 Relocation Act partition (Hopi Partitioned Lands, Big Mountain) still drives resentment. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-111publ18/pdf/PLAW-111publ18.pdf ; https://navajotimes.com/news/2009/0509/051409freeze.php
2. **Little Colorado River water rights.**
   - The general stream adjudication (CV-6417, Apache County Superior Court; Judge **Eddward Ballinger Jr.**, Special Master **George A. Schade Jr.**) was active in 2009.
   - On Feb 27, 2009 the court ruled the Hopi cannot claim water sources on Navajo land.
   - The Navajo–Hopi–state settlement had been negotiated since March 2008. The Navajo Council approved it Nov 2010; it failed 2012–13.
   - Catalyst Paper (Snowflake) was a litigant.
   - **WMAT Water Rights Quantification Act of 2009** was pending in Congress, with Chairman Lupe testifying. Enactment in Dec 2010 is general knowledge, **UNVERIFIED** here.
   - https://www.superiorcourt.maricopa.gov/SuperiorCourt/GeneralStreamAdjudication/docs/ballinger-LCRAHopiord030209.pdf ; https://www.superiorcourt.maricopa.gov/SuperiorCourt/GeneralStreamAdjudication/docs/schade-HopiPriorityme051710.pdf
3. **Rodeo–Chediski Fire (June 18 to July 7, 2002).**
   - Burned 468,638 acres and destroyed about 426–491 structures.
   - **30,000+ evacuated**, including all of Show Low (~7,700), Pinetop-Lakeside, Heber-Overgaard (268 buildings lost) and Linden (~100 homes).
   - The Rodeo Fire was set by White Mountain Apache contract firefighter **Leonard Gregg** to get work. The Chediski Fire was a signal fire from lost hiker **Valinda Jo Elliott**.
   - About 60% of the damage was on tribal land, and it was Arizona's largest fire until 2011.
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodeo%E2%80%93Chediski_Fire ; https://www.azdhs.gov/documents/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/environmental-toxicology/rodeo_chedeski_assmnt.pdf
4. **Uranium legacy.** About 30 million tons of uranium ore were mined on Navajo land 1944–1986, leaving 500+ abandoned mines plus contaminated homes and water. A federal five-year multi-agency cleanup plan started in 2008. https://www.epa.gov/navajo-nation-uranium-cleanup/aum-cleanup ; https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2025-06/Navajo%20Nation%20UMTRCA%20Communications%20and%20Outreach%20Plan.pdf
5. **2009 H1N1 locally.**
   - First Arizona-side Navajo cases were in May 2009.
   - Kayenta was "caught by surprise" at the end of the 2008-09 school year, and field trips were cancelled. (https://kayentatownship-nsn.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/9_09.pdf)
   - Navajo Division of Health / NAIHS mass vaccination on **Oct 7, 2009** used 11 dispensing sites and gave about 17,000 shots. H1N1 vaccine was scarce in Oct–Nov.
   - A BIE school on the NM side of the Navajo Nation closed in Sept 2009.
   - CDC (Dec 2009): AI/AN H1N1 death rate was 4× other groups. Flagstaff Medical Center's ICU was mostly Native ventilator patients.
   - The ADHS weekly report for Oct 11–17, 2009 showed **16 confirmed H1N1 in Navajo County** that week (secondary source: https://flutrackers.com/threads/summary-of-arizona-dept-of-health.66654/).
   - https://h1n1.navajo-nsn.gov/ ; https://www.cdc.gov/Mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5848a1.htm ; https://indianz.com/News/2009/09/24/bie_school_closed_for_large_nu.asp
6. **Navajo Nation curfews.** No 2009 curfew was found. The famous Navajo weekend curfews and lockdowns were COVID-19 (2020), so they are **not applicable to 2009**. Any 2009-era curfew: **UNVERIFIED**.
7. **Alcohol.**
   - The Navajo Nation Code (Title 17) bans the sale, possession and consumption of alcohol. The only exceptions are Antelope Point Marina (2001) and, from 2008, a narrowly approved (38–36) exception for dining at the new Fire Rock casino near Gallup. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2008/12/24/E8-30688/navajo-nation-liquor-regulations ; https://tucson.com/news/state-and-regional/article_00955f6b-0074-562b-a91b-7f68ebc00882.html
   - The Hopi reservation is also dry (general knowledge, **UNVERIFIED**).
   - **Border towns**: Winslow, Holbrook, Gallup and Farmington are alcohol magnets with bootlegging, exposure deaths and "drunk bars." Former Winslow mayor Affeldt cited bars and pushed for a detox center. Holbrook and Winslow held public-intoxication summits in 2011. https://ictnews.org/archive/drinking-off-and-on-reservations-a-huge-problem-in-southwest ; https://www.tribunenewsnow.com/area-officials-tackle-public-intoxication-once-again/
   - Navajo Nation Human Rights Commission border-town race-relations hearings ran in 2008–2010. https://nnhrc.navajo-nsn.gov/Portals/0/Files/071810_Assessing_Race_Relations_Between_Navajos_and_Non-Navajos.pdf?ver
8. **Jurisdiction gaps.** County deputies historically stayed off the reservation. The **Nov 2009 cross-deputization deal** (Sheriff Clark, County Attorney Carlyon, NN Public Safety Director Cowboy, Acting Chief Etsitty) planned deputies stationed in **Kayenta and Piñon**. https://navajotimes.com/news/2009/1109/112509deputies.php
9. **Infrastructure fragility.**
   - Southern chapter wells ran dry in July 2009 (Dilkon, Teesto, Jeddito, Whitecone).
   - The FEMA-decertified Winslow Levee put a large share of Winslow in the floodplain, and insurance costs rose.
   - Many reservation homes lack water and power (general knowledge).
10. **Coal politics.** Both tribes depend on coal revenue (Peabody on Black Mesa; Kayenta Mine). Hopi and Navajo leaders sided with coal against environmental groups (Sept 2009 ban). The Cholla and Coronado plants and the Catalyst mill employ the off-reservation workforce. https://www.hcn.org/issues/41-18/indians-vs-greens/
11. **Recession.** County unemployment peaked around 16% (Oct 2009: 15.7%), and Kayenta was around 21% (2009 CHSA figure). Catalyst rolled layoffs through the year. Snowflake and Taylor explored merging the two towns. http://hdl.handle.net/2286/R.I.23702
12. **Mormon pioneer culture in Snowflake/Taylor.** 1878 settlement, the temple (2002), church welfare ranches, "Firing of the Anvil" on the 4th of July in Taylor, the Pioneer Days Festival, and Republican politics (Sen. Allen).
13. **Show Low tourism and second homes.** The White Mountains are a summer refuge for Phoenix residents ("90,000 permanent and seasonal residents" per Summit Healthcare), with Hon-Dah casino, Sunrise ski area and Fool Hollow Lake. Rodeo-Chediski made fire risk a political issue. Heavy second-home exposure to the 2008–09 housing crash is general knowledge, **UNVERIFIED** with data.
