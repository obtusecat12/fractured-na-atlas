The Federal Government of the United States, usually called the Sacramento government in news reports since the Presidents' Day Massacre, is the constitutional authority that claims to be the sole legitimate government of the United States of America. On February 16, 2009 the President was killed at the White House, and the Vice President, the Speaker of the House and the President pro tempore of the Senate were killed in the attack on the joint session of Congress. That night Attorney General Raymond E. Calloway, the designated survivor, was sworn in as Acting President at an undisclosed location under the Presidential Succession Act of 1947. The government moved to Sacramento, California in early March.

As of November 2009 the federal government controls most of California, southern and central Arizona, a corridor to Las Vegas, Alaska, Hawaii, the Nueces Strip of southern Texas and the besieged enclave of El Paso (Fort Bliss). It is recognized as the lawful government of the United States by Canada, the member states of the European Union, Japan, Australia and most other countries, and keeps the U.S. seat on the United Nations Security Council.[7]

[edit]Succession

[edit]February 16

The joint session had been called to hear the President's address on the "national fertility emergency", which was due to begin at 9 p.m. Eastern Time. At about 8:40 p.m. a bomb exploded in the West Wing of the White House, where the President was waiting to leave for the Capitol; he was pronounced dead at the scene.[1] Within minutes gunmen dressed in Capitol Police uniforms entered the House chamber. The Vice President, the Speaker and the President pro tempore were among those killed. The attacks were officially blamed on "Islamic fanatics", a claim that has never been substantiated.[citation needed]

Under long-standing practice one Cabinet member is kept away from Washington during a joint session. That night it was Attorney General Calloway, who had been flown to a site that the government has still not named; reports have placed it at Mount Weather in Virginia or at Offutt Air Force Base in Nebraska.[2] The Secretary of State, the Secretary of the Treasury and the Secretary of Defense, who come before the Attorney General in the line of succession, were in the chamber and were killed or are missing. Calloway took the oath from a federal district judge at about 11:10 p.m. Eastern Time. No recording of the oath has been released.

[edit]Break with the command in Washington

On February 17 the Army command in the National Capital Region declared a national emergency, "temporarily suspended" the Constitution and announced that it would not recognize Calloway's succession. Calloway's office called the declaration "without any constitutional basis" and ordered all federal personnel to disregard it. Over the next weeks most commands west of the Mississippi, including United States Pacific Command and the I Marine Expeditionary Force, stated that they took orders from the Acting President; commands on the East Coast mostly did not.[3]

The split became permanent in April, when the Sons of Jacob provisional government in Washington proclaimed the Republic of Gilead.

[edit]Relocation to Sacramento

The government left the Midwest site in early March 2009. Sacramento was chosen over San Diego and Denver; according to the Sacramento Bee, the deciding factors were the state government buildings already in place, Travis Air Force Base about 45 miles away and the then-governor's offer of the California State Capitol.[4] The Acting President's office occupies the east annex of the Capitol, and his residence is at the former McClellan Air Force Base in North Highlands. Several departments work out of the Robert T. Matsui United States Courthouse and leased office space on Capitol Mall.

[edit]Western mainland

The mainland part runs from the Butte–Sutter line in the north, agreed with the State of Jefferson in the Red Bluff Accord of June 2009, to the Mexican border at San Diego and Nogales. It includes the San Francisco Bay Area, the Central Valley, Southern California, Phoenix and Tucson. A corridor along Interstate 15 reaches Las Vegas and St. George; it is held mainly by Nevada National Guard units and has been cut several times. Northern coastal California is held by Cascadia and Jefferson. Kingman lies east of Route 66, outside the federal line.

[edit]Alaska and Hawaii

Alaska has been under military administration since March 2009, after the state legislature in Juneau failed to reach a quorum. The district is run jointly from Elmendorf Air Force Base and the neighbouring Fort Richardson in Anchorage. The Trans-Alaska Pipeline has kept running, and its oil now goes almost entirely to federal territory through Valdez.[9]

In Hawaii the governor and legislature still sit in Honolulu, but civil defence, ports and air traffic were put under United States Pacific Command at Camp H. M. Smith in April. The arrangement is described by the federal government as temporary.[citation needed]

[edit]South Texas and El Paso

The South Texas Federal Protectorate covers the counties south of the Nueces River, including Nueces (322,077), Cameron (392,736), Hidalgo (726,604) and Webb (236,941). It was formed in May 2009 when federal forces at Naval Air Station Corpus Christi and Naval Air Station Kingsville refused to come under the Texas provisional government.

El Paso has been surrounded on three sides by the Mexican Occupation Zone since June. Biggs Army Airfield is the only working way in or out; the airlift from Kirtland stopped in July and flights now come from Davis–Monthan near Tucson.[10]

[edit]Government and politics

[edit]Executive

Calloway has not named a Vice President. Under the Twenty-fifth Amendment a nominee would need confirmation by both houses, and the government has said it will not put forward a name until the Interim Congress's status is settled.[5] The Cabinet is made up of surviving deputy secretaries serving in an acting capacity. Calloway gives a weekly radio address from the Capitol, carried by Armed Forces Network and most stations in federal territory.

[edit]Interim Congress

Both houses lost most of their members on February 16. In May 2009 surviving members from western states and senators appointed under state law formed an Interim Congress in the Sacramento Memorial Auditorium. On paper it has 23 senators and 61 representatives.[clarification needed] Whether this is a quorum, and so whether it can lawfully legislate, is disputed among legal scholars; the government's position is that a quorum is a majority of members "duly chosen and sworn" who are still living.[5]

[edit]Emergency powers

The government acts under the National Emergencies Act and the Stafford Act. Night curfews are in force in Los Angeles, Oakland and Phoenix. In September 2009 the Federal Communications Commission issued Emergency Directive 09-4A under the Domestic Security and Communications Act, ordering search engines to suspend indexing of some content. The American Civil Liberties Union has filed suit in the Northern District of California.[6]

[edit]Military

The government kept most of the United States Pacific Fleet at San Diego and Pearl Harbor, the I Marine Expeditionary Force at Camp Pendleton, Edwards, Vandenberg and the National Guard of California and Arizona. Fort Bliss and its 1st Armored Division units are cut off. Most of the Atlantic Fleet stayed in port at Norfolk and is believed to be under Gilead's control.[citation needed]

[edit]Nuclear detonations

Main article: 2009 North American nuclear explosions

Five nuclear devices have exploded in North America in 2009. Four of them went off in August: at the Concord Naval Weapons Station and the Mare Island Naval Shipyard on the north side of San Francisco Bay, at Kingman, and at the former Marine Corps Air Station El Toro in Orange County. Concord, Mare Island and El Toro were inside federal territory, and Travis Air Force Base was badly damaged.

The fifth went off on November 4 at 3:41 p.m. CST near Millington, about 15 miles north of Memphis, close to Naval Support Activity Mid-South. Officials put the yield at 10 to 15 kilotons. Everyone within 30 miles was told to shelter in place, and Tennessee, Arkansas and Mississippi declared emergencies; Shelby County alone had an estimated 906,825 residents in 2008.[11] Millington is outside federal territory. Less than an hour after the blast Calloway told reporters at the Capitol that federal investigators were "already on the ground in Tennessee" and said, "We will find who did this."[12]

No group has credibly claimed any of the five detonations. The federal government has blamed Gilead for the August attacks; Gilead has blamed "the Sacramento usurpers".[13]

[edit]Economy

This section requires expansion.

The Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco has acted as the central bank since March, after the Federal Reserve Board in Washington stopped functioning. The dollar is still used in nearly every part of the former United States. Federal revenue has fallen sharply; the Internal Revenue Service is collecting only from returns filed in the West.[citation needed]

[edit]Foreign relations

  • Canada: recognized the Sacramento government on March 12 and has opened its border to refugees; the U.S. embassy in Ottawa has stayed open.[7]
  • European Union: member states generally recognize Sacramento and have frozen overseas assets controlled from Washington.
  • Mexico: diplomatic relations maintained, but strained over the Mexican Occupation Zone and the siege of Fort Bliss.
  • Japan and Australia: recognize Sacramento; both have said they will honour treaty commitments.
  • Republic of Gilead: no mutual recognition; a de facto state of war.
