Sequoyah is a state in the South Central United States. It is bordered by Oklahoma to the west, Kansas and Missouri to the north, Arkansas to the east, and Texas across the Red River to the south. Its territory roughly matches the former Indian Territory, with a total area of about 34,000 square miles. Its population was 1,777,157 at the 2000 census. The capital is Muskogee and the largest city is Tulsa.

The state is named after Sequoyah, who created the Cherokee syllabary. In 1905 leaders of the Five Civilized Tribes met at Muskogee in the Sequoyah Constitutional Convention and drafted a state constitution. Sequoyah was admitted to the Union on November 16, 1907, on the same day as Oklahoma.[1]

Since the 2009 crisis the area under the state government's control has shrunk to fifteen counties in the north-east and Pawnee County in Oklahoma. Contact with the southern counties has been lost.

[edit]History

[edit]Indian Territory

After the Indian Removal Act of 1830, the Cherokee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Creek and Seminole were forced to move from the south-eastern United States to land west of the Mississippi. The Cherokee call the journey the Trail of Tears. In the new territory each nation set up its own government, courts and schools; the Cherokee capital was at Tahlequah.

The Curtis Act of 1898 set March 4, 1906 as the date for dissolving the tribal governments and dividing tribal land among individual members. Faced with the prospect of being joined to Oklahoma Territory to the west, the leaders of the five nations began after 1903 to support separate statehood for Indian Territory.

[edit]Sequoyah Convention

In July 1905, at the urging of Cherokee citizen James A. Norman, Cherokee Principal Chief William C. Rogers and Choctaw Principal Chief Green McCurtain issued a call for a convention. It opened at the Hinton Theater in Muskogee on August 21. Creek Principal Chief Pleasant Porter was chairman and the Creek poet Alexander Posey was secretary.[2] More than 150 delegates drafted a constitution of about 35,000 words, drew county lines and designed a state seal.

In the referendum of November 7 the constitution passed by 56,279 votes to 9,073.[2] In his annual message of December 5, President Theodore Roosevelt called for the two territories to be admitted as a single state.[3]

[edit]Separate statehood

In the spring of 1906 the Senate, working from an amendment offered by Porter J. McCumber, changed the Enabling Act to let the voters of Indian Territory decide separately whether to enter the Union as a state under the Sequoyah constitution, much like the choice given to Arizona at the same time.[citation needed] Roosevelt signed the act on June 16. That November a majority of Indian Territory voters chose separate statehood, and the Osage reservation was included in the new state. On November 16, 1907 Roosevelt proclaimed Sequoyah and Oklahoma admitted to the Union.[4]

Critics said the main effect was to add two Democratic seats to the Senate.[5] A 1910 referendum made Muskogee the capital.

[edit]20th century

After oil was struck at Red Fork in 1901, Tulsa grew into the "Oil Capital of the World". In the Tulsa race massacre of 1921 a white mob burned the Greenwood district. The Grand River Dam Authority completed Pensacola Dam in 1940, forming Grand Lake o' the Cherokees. From the 1970s the five nations again elected their own governments. American Airlines runs its largest maintenance base at Tulsa International Airport.

[edit]2009 crisis

On February 17 Governor Fourkiller declared a state of emergency. The Sequoyah National Guard assembled at Camp Gruber and took over Tulsa International Airport and the toll plazas on Interstate 44.[6] In March the Oklahoma state government in Oklahoma City stopped broadcasting.

From April the state lost contact with McAlester, Durant and the other southern counties, and the whereabouts of the Choctaw Nation and Chickasaw Nation governments are unknown. In May the Pawnee County commission asked to be administered by Sequoyah, and the Guard moved in to protect Keystone Lake and Keystone Dam.[7]

The state government relies on hydroelectric power from the Grand River Dam Authority and fuel from the Tulsa refineries, and runs a joint relief center with the Cherokee Nation government at Tahlequah. The Tulsa World reported that by October about 62,000 evacuees from the southern counties and Kansas had registered.[8]

[edit]Geography

The east of the state is the Ozark Plateau and the Ouachita Mountains; the west is the Osage Hills and prairie. The Arkansas River runs from north-west to south-east through Tulsa and Muskogee, and the Grand and Illinois rivers join it from the north-east.

[edit]Flag and seal

The seal was designed by the 1905 convention and adopted as the state seal in 1907. Each point of the large central star holds the emblem of one nation: the Cherokee seven-pointed star in a wreath of oak leaves, the Creek plow and sheaf of wheat, the Seminole hunter in a canoe, the Chickasaw warrior, and the Choctaw tomahawk, bow and arrows. Above the star sits Sequoyah holding a tablet of his syllabary.[9] The flag is dark blue with the seal in the center and the name of the state below it.[citation needed]
