The Republic of Gilead, officially the Divine Republic of Gilead, is a regime proclaimed in Washington, D.C. in April 2009 by the "Sons of Jacob", a military-religious movement that seized the federal capital after the Presidents' Day Massacre. As of November 2009 Gilead controls the Interstate 95 urban corridor of the Northeastern United States, from Northern Virginia through Baltimore, Philadelphia and New York City to metropolitan Boston, and most of the rest of New England, together with three districts in northern Maine, New Hampshire and northeastern Vermont which it governs separately.

Gilead is not recognized by any member state of the United Nations. The U.S. federal government in Sacramento, Canada and the European Union regard it as an illegal occupying authority. The Holy See recalled its nuncio from Washington after Gilead declared the Catholic Church a "hostile sect". Because Gilead does not admit foreign journalists and has cut international telephone lines, most of what is known about it comes from refugee testimony, documents smuggled out to Canada, and monitored broadcasts, and many details below are disputed.

[edit]Name

"Gilead" is taken from the region of Gilead east of the Jordan River in the Hebrew Bible; Jeremiah 8:22 asks "Is there no balm in Gilead; is there no physician there?".[8] The name first appears in mimeographed pamphlets circulated among groups linked to the Sons of Jacob in 2006–2007.[1] The regime's own broadcasts use "the Republic of Gilead", "Gilead" and, in edicts, "the Divine Republic". Canadian government documents use "the Gilead authorities" or "the de facto authorities in Washington". Refugees interviewed in New Brunswick said that the words "Under His Eye" are used as an everyday greeting and farewell in Gilead-held towns.[7]

[edit]History

[edit]Background

Between 2003 and 2008 birth rates fell steadily in the United States and most industrialized countries. From 2006 the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention used the term "Birth Dearth" in its reports.[2] Outbreaks of a drug-resistant strain of syphilis (dubbed "R-strain" by the press) and scares over chemical contamination and nuclear plant accidents added to public unease.

"Sons of Jacob" is a collective name for a network of conservative think tanks and churches that took shape around 2005, advocating a "restoration of the traditional family order" as the answer to the fertility crisis. Its members included retired officers, private security contractors and a number of state legislators, but it had little influence in national politics before 2009.[3] According to The Atlantic, the network held closed meetings at rented conference centres in Northern Virginia and Maryland from 2006, at which position papers on "fertile women as a national resource" were circulated. Several participants later said they took the papers to be a thought exercise.[3][citation needed]

[edit]Presidents' Day Massacre

Main article: Presidents' Day Massacre

On 16 February 2009 (Presidents' Day) the White House was bombed, and gunmen opened fire on a joint session of Congress convened to hear the President's address on a "national fertility emergency". The President, the Vice President and most members present were killed. The attack was initially blamed on "Islamic fanatics", a claim that no independent inquiry has confirmed.[4] The designated survivor, Attorney General Raymond E. Calloway, was sworn in as Acting President that night at an undisclosed location, and moved the federal government to Sacramento in early March.

On 17 February the military in Washington declared a national emergency and "temporarily suspended" the Constitution, imposing a curfew and press censorship and introducing the Identipass. The BBC reported in May that several officers who signed the 17 February declaration were later identified as members of Sons of Jacob study groups, and that the speed with which checkpoints went up around the Capital Beltway suggested advance planning.[4]

[edit]Compucard freeze

In March 2009 the provisional authorities froze all bank accounts and Compucards held by women, transferring the funds to husbands or male next of kin, and ordered women dismissed from employment. The Associated Press reported that the order took effect overnight on 9–10 March, and that women in Washington and Baltimore found their cards refused at shops the next morning; accounts were reportedly identified by a sex code already held in the Compucard database.[11] The Sacramento government declared the directive void on 23 March, but it continued to be applied wherever the Washington authorities controlled the banks.

Protest marches in Boston and Washington were fired upon.[5] In Boston, troops opened fire on marchers on Boylston Street on 20 March. The number of dead has never been established; figures given by witnesses to the Boston Globe ranged from "a dozen" to more than forty.[12] A march toward the Capitol in Washington the following weekend was dispersed with tear gas and live rounds near Union Station.

[edit]Proclamation and "the War"

In April 2009 the provisional government proclaimed the Republic of Gilead in Washington and named the Quakers, Catholics and Baptists "hostile sects". Most sources give 7 April as the date of the proclamation, read on what had been WTOP radio; some refugee accounts put it a day earlier.[citation needed] Through the spring and summer Gilead fought on two fronts, which its broadcasts together call "the War".

[edit]Appalachian front

Gilead's forces in the west are styled the 4th Division, "Angels of the Apocalypse" (apparently unrelated to the U.S. Army's 4th Infantry Division at Fort Hood). From late April they fought Baptist militias in the Appalachian Mountains, first along the Blue Ridge passes west of Loudoun County: Snickers Gap on Route 7, and the Interstate 66 approach to Front Royal.[16] Gilead radio repeatedly announced that the Angels were "smoking out" pockets of Baptist guerrillas in the "Appalachian Highlands" with air support. The guerrillas are now usually identified with the Appalachian Guerrilla Government in the Shenandoah Valley and the West Virginia Baptist Convention further west. By August the front had settled roughly along the Loudoun–Clarke county line and the Blue Ridge crest, and Skyline Drive in Shenandoah National Park was reported closed and mined. Casualty figures from both sides cannot be checked.

Gilead broadcasts also claimed that a "21st Battalion, Angels of Light" was fighting in Detroit in May. Street fighting was reported there, but Detroit is now within the Radio Silence zone and it is not clear that any Gilead unit was involved; most analysts treat the claim as propaganda.[citation needed]

[edit]I-95 advance

The second column advanced north along I-95. The order of events, mostly from wire reports, was:

  • Late April: Baltimore secured; the Fort McHenry Tunnel closed to civilian traffic. Fort Meade and Aberdeen Proving Ground were reported to have come over without fighting.[citation needed]
  • 2 May: Gilead troops at the Delaware Memorial Bridge; Wilmington taken shortly afterwards.
  • 9–16 May: Fighting in and around Philadelphia. Reuters reported armoured vehicles on I-95 near the airport on 11 May and gunfire in Kensington for several days.[13] The Pennsylvania National Guard units in the city withdrew west toward Harrisburg.
  • Late May: Advance up the New Jersey Turnpike; Trenton and New Brunswick occupied. Large numbers of residents fled west on I-78 and I-80.
  • 29 May – 21 June: Battle for New York City. Gilead units crossed from Staten Island and from Fort Lee, where the George Washington Bridge was closed after an explosion on its upper level on 4 June. Elements of the 42nd Infantry Division of the New York Army National Guard held parts of the Bronx until mid-June before pulling back north.[14] Manhattan was declared "pacified" on 21 June. One estimate is that more than 1.5 million people left the city during the fighting.[citation needed]
  • Early July: Long Island occupied through Nassau and Suffolk counties. Gilead troops moved along I-95 through Fairfield County and New Haven; Naval Submarine Base New London at Groton was evacuated by sea before they arrived.[citation needed]
  • 14 July: Providence occupied.
  • 18–26 July: Boston. Fighting was reported along Route 128 and in Dorchester; Hanscom Air Force Base fell on about 22 July. After the city was taken, several ministries were moved into the buildings of Harvard University in Cambridge.
  • Early August: The advance south of Boston stopped on the Norfolk–Bristol county line around Foxborough and Mansfield, where it met the Blackwater Security Concession.

[edit]Northern Maine and the Northeast Kingdom

Gilead's hold on northern New England came mostly without battles. In August church councils in Bangor and Presque Isle declared for "the provisional authority in Washington", and on 14 August the Bangor Daily News reported that a convoy had reached Bangor on I-95 the previous day.[17] The Aroostook March and the Penobscot Covenant District were set up over the following weeks. Gilead troops reached Houlton, where I-95 ends at the Canadian border, in late August.[28] In the Northeast Kingdom of Vermont, Gilead took St. Johnsbury in early September, and with it the Interstate 91 crossing at Derby Line.

[edit]Resettlement of the Children of Ham

Main article: Resettlement of the Children of Ham

In July 2009 Gilead announced the "Resettlement of the Children of Ham" to "National Homeland One" in North Dakota. The first announcement monitored by the BBC Monitoring service was broadcast on 22 July and said that resettlement was "proceeding on schedule" and that "three thousand a day" were being moved.[29] The ACLU and several international human rights groups have called it a forced deportation of African Americans, and on 4 August the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights called on Gilead to stop it.[6]

Refugees have described roundups in Baltimore, Prince George's County, Philadelphia and Newark, and a holding camp at Fort Dix in Burlington County. Where the trains go is not known. North Dakota is not under Gilead control, officials in North Dakota have said no such trains have arrived, and the Sacramento government says "National Homeland One" does not exist.[citation needed] Some human rights groups fear the deportees are being held in camps inside Gilead or have been killed; Gilead has not allowed the International Committee of the Red Cross to visit.

[edit]Jewish repatriation boats

Gilead also began a "repatriation" scheme for Jews, who were reportedly given a choice between conversion and emigration to Israel. Ships chartered by Gilead left Port Newark and Baltimore from July. Gilead radio claimed in October that 19,000 people had departed. Israel's Ministry of Immigrant Absorption said on 20 October that it had registered about 6,800 arrivals at Haifa from Gilead ports, which would leave a large number unaccounted for.[20] One ship, the Panamanian-flagged Atlantic Dawn, with about 400 passengers, stopped answering radio calls west of the Azores in September and has not been found.[21] Some arrivals in Haifa said other passengers had been put into boats at sea; this has not been verified.

[edit]Relations with northeastern neighbours

Southeastern Massachusetts (Plymouth County to Nantucket) and the Rhode Island counties of Bristol and Newport are held by the Blackwater Security Concession, which faces Gilead along the line south of I-95. The line has changed hands several times; in September both sides claimed the Attleboro rail yard. There is no known agreement between them, though trucks have been reported crossing at Route 24.[citation needed]

The Republic of Vermont and the Green Mountain Republic reject Gilead's claim to Vermont's three northeastern counties. The Republic of Vermont closed U.S. Route 2 east of Danville in September and says it is sheltering several thousand people from the Kingdom in Montpelier and Barre.

[edit]Geography

See also: Interstate 95, Northeast Corridor

Gilead's territory falls into two very different parts. The southern core is the densely built Northeast megalopolis along I-95 and the Northeast Corridor railway, from the Potomac River to the Boston basin, with the Chesapeake and Delaware bays and the Hudson estuary. Gilead also claims the Delmarva Peninsula counties of Delaware and the Eastern Shore of Virginia (Accomack and Northampton), most of Maryland east of Frederick County, all of New Jersey, Connecticut and New Hampshire, and Maine south of the Penobscot district. Control of rural western Massachusetts and of interior Maine is said to be thin, limited to the main roads and the larger towns; estimates of the area under effective control (c. 124,500 km²) are about two thirds of the area claimed.[citation needed]

The northern part is three thinly populated districts, administered separately from Washington and described in Gilead broadcasts as "Marches" or "Covenant Districts".

[edit]Northern districts

Aroostook March (Aroostook County, seat Presque Isle) is the largest county east of the Mississippi River and borders New Brunswick and Quebec on two sides. Its economy rests on potatoes: about 90 percent of Maine's crop is grown here, and Maine farmers harvested an estimated 54,500 acres in 2008.[25] The former Loring Air Force Base at Limestone, closed in 1994, is reported to be in use as a Gilead depot.[citation needed] The border crossings at Houlton–Woodstock, Madawaska–Edmundston and Fort Kent–Clair were the main refugee routes into New Brunswick until Gilead closed them in September.

Penobscot Covenant District (Penobscot and Piscataquis counties, seat Bangor, pop. 31,473 in 2000) covers the Penobscot basin and the timberlands around Moosehead Lake. Its paper mills at Millinocket and East Millinocket had largely shut before the crisis. Bangor International Airport is reported to be in military use.

District of the Northeast Kingdom (Orleans, Caledonia and Essex counties in Vermont and Coös County in New Hampshire, seat St. Johnsbury) lies on the upper Connecticut River north of the White Mountains. It is an exclave in the sense that the only road link to the rest of Gilead is U.S. 3 and I-93 through Franconia Notch.

[edit]Military installations

Bases reported under Gilead control include Andrews Air Force Base, Fort Belvoir, Marine Corps Base Quantico, Fort Meade, Aberdeen Proving Ground, the United States Naval Academy at Annapolis, McGuire Air Force Base and Fort Dix, Hanscom Air Force Base and Portsmouth Naval Shipyard at Kittery. The status of Naval Air Station Brunswick is unclear.[citation needed]

[edit]Politics

Gilead has no written constitution. Power rests with the Council of the Sons of Jacob, which governs by edict. B. Frederick Judd is described in broadcasts as Chairman of the Provisional Supreme Council; he has been seen in public only a handful of times, and no biography has been published.[3] Frederick R. Waterford is said by The Globe and Mail's sources to have been responsible for the regime's dress rules and public ceremonies, and to have worked in market research before 2009.[7][who?] Joseph Lawrence, reportedly a former university economist, is credited in leaked papers with the economic plan and with the labour camps known as the "Colonies".[7]

According to leaked internal documents, society is ranked by male titles ("Commander", "Guardian", "Angel") and assigned female roles.[7] Refugee accounts collected by Canadian agencies describe the following, though the terms and their meanings vary between accounts:

  • Commanders of the Faithful: senior officials, allotted large houses in Washington, Chevy Chase and Cambridge.
  • Angels: soldiers at the fronts. Guardians of the Faith: police and household guards.
  • Eyes: the secret police. Refugees said they used black vans.
  • Wives: wives of Commanders. Econowives: wives of lower-ranking men.
  • Handmaids: women who had borne children, assigned to Commanders' households to bear children for them. This is the most disputed point in the leaked papers; Gilead's broadcasts have never used the word.
  • Marthas: domestic servants. Aunts: women who train and supervise the Handmaids, reportedly at a centre in a former school gymnasium.
  • Unwomen: women who refuse an assigned role, and who are said to be sent to the Colonies.

Public executions, which refugees call "Salvagings", have been reported in Boston, and bodies are said to be displayed on the wall around Harvard Yard.[citation needed] Because of press controls, outside knowledge of conditions in Gilead comes mainly from refugee testimony and interviews by Canadian refugee agencies.

[edit]Religion

The April proclamation named Quakers, Catholics and Baptists as hostile sects. Catholic churches in Philadelphia and Boston were reported closed in May and June, and priests arrested. The position of Baptists is confused: Gilead fights Baptist guerrillas in Appalachia, but its northern Maine districts were set up with the help of local evangelical and independent Baptist churches. Several refugees told CBC News that membership in "approved congregations" decided who received ration cards in Aroostook County.[15]

[edit]Underground Femaleroad and Quaker smuggling

Refugees and Canadian aid workers describe an escape network that refugees call the "Underground Femaleroad", after the Underground Railroad of the 19th century, in which Quakers in Philadelphia had also played a large part.[24] Quaker meetings in Pennsylvania, New Jersey and New England are said to have hidden women and moved them north in stages, using farm trucks and logging roads. Gilead's broadcasts call it the work of "Quaker traitors"; at least eleven people were hanged in Philadelphia in June for "smuggling of women", according to the BBC.[4]

The main routes reported are:

  • Through the woods of western Maine to Jackman and across the border to Armstrong on U.S. Route 201.
  • Through the Northeast Kingdom to Stanstead and the Eastern Townships, before Derby Line was taken.
  • By small boat from the Down East coast of Washington County to Campobello Island and Grand Manan, and on to Yarmouth. Gilead closed the FDR International Bridge between Lubec and Campobello in August.

The Canadian Friends Service Committee in Toronto has said it is in contact with the network but has given no details. Some refugees also mention "Mayday" as a name for an armed resistance group in Gilead; nothing is known about it.[citation needed]

[edit]Refugees

Main article: Gilead refugee crisis in Canada

Canada closed its border crossings with Gilead-held territory but opened refugee channels. According to Citizenship and Immigration Canada, 212,400 people who had left Gilead-held territory were registered in Canada by 31 October 2009.[18] The UNHCR said in October that the true number could be "closer to 300,000", since many crossed without registering or reached Ontario through upstate New York, which Gilead does not control.[19] About two thirds of adult arrivals are women.

The tent camp at CFB Gagetown passed 30,000 residents in early October, according to the Telegraph-Journal.[23] La Presse reported in September that Valcartier was being winterized.[22] In Toronto, the Toronto Star has described an area of rooming houses and church halls taken over by arrivals, which residents have started to call "Little America".[30]

[edit]Economy

This section requires expansion.

Little reliable information is available. Container traffic at Port Newark–Elizabeth and the Port of Baltimore is reported to have mostly stopped, and the Northeast Corridor railway is used for military traffic. Women may not hold property or money; all wages are paid into men's Compucard accounts. Rationing of fuel and food was reported in New York and Philadelphia from July.[citation needed] Refugees say the Colonies are camps where women and men classed as Unwomen or "Gender Traitors" clean up contaminated industrial sites.

[edit]Flag and symbols

The flag first appeared in footage from Washington in May 2009. It keeps the thirteen red and white stripes of the U.S. flag, but the canton carries a ring of white stars around an Eye of Providence on a blue field. The motto "Under His Eye" appears on the seal used on edicts.
