# State of Superior

> 【模板:现正发生】This article documents a **current event**. Information may change rapidly as the event progresses, and initial news reports may be unreliable.
> 【模板:消歧义】This article is about the self-governing state formed in 2009. For the historical statehood proposals, see [[Superior (proposed U.S. state)]]. For the lake, see [[Lake Superior]].

The **State of Superior** is a de facto self-governing state formed after the [[Presidents' Day Massacre]] by the fifteen counties of the [[Upper Peninsula of Michigan]], with its capital at [[Marquette, Michigan|Marquette]]. The peninsula has sought to leave [[Michigan]] and form its own state since 1858, and the name comes from [[Lake Superior]] to the north. In March 2009 the emergency district set up there by the [[Federal Emergency Management Agency]] (FEMA) stopped functioning; county delegates proclaimed the state at the Marquette County Courthouse on March 27 and took over the district's supplies and facilities.

Superior does not claim to have left the Union, but it does not recognize the state government in [[Lansing, Michigan|Lansing]]. The north end of the [[Mackinac Bridge]] has been closed since March 2009, and most outside traffic now runs over the [[Sault Ste. Marie International Bridge|International Bridge]] at [[Sault Ste. Marie, Michigan|Sault Ste. Marie]] to [[Ontario]], [[Canada]]. As of November 2009 the [[Federal Government of the United States (2009–present)|federal government]] had not recognized its statehood.

## Name

The name refers to Lake Superior and was taken from the 1858 proposal, which also used the alternative "State of Ontonagon".<ref>1</ref> During the March 2009 convention a few delegates from [[Gogebic County, Michigan|Gogebic]] and [[Ontonagon County, Michigan|Ontonagon]] counties proposed "Ontonagon", and at least one delegate suggested "Sylvania", the name [[Thomas Jefferson]] had proposed in 1784 for a state in roughly the same area.{{citation needed}} Residents of the peninsula are commonly called "[[Yooper]]s", from "U.P.-ers", and the informal nickname "Yooper Country" appears on hand-painted signs at the St. Ignace checkpoint.

## History

### Earlier statehood proposals

{{Main|Superior (proposed U.S. state)}}

The Upper Peninsula was not part of the territory Michigan originally claimed. It was given to Michigan by Congress in 1836 as compensation for losing the [[Toledo Strip]] to [[Ohio]] after the [[Toledo War]], and Michigan was admitted to the Union in January 1837.

In 1858 a convention at [[Ontonagon, Michigan|Ontonagon]] proposed combining the Upper Peninsula, northern [[Wisconsin]] and northeastern [[Minnesota]] into a new state called "Superior" or "Ontonagon".<ref>2</ref> *[[The New York Times]]* wrote at the time:

>> Unless Congress should interpose objections, which cannot reasonably be apprehended, we see no cause why the new "State of Ontonagon" should not speedily take her place as an independent member of the union.<ref>1</ref>

Another proposal in 1897 included parts of Wisconsin.<ref>3</ref> In 1959, after the admission of [[Alaska]] and [[Hawaii]], a resident of [[Ironwood, Michigan|Ironwood]] filed a mock lawsuit asking for a "divorce" between the two peninsulas. An Upper Peninsula Independence Association was founded in 1962; a secession bill went to the [[Michigan Legislature]], and a petition for a referendum gathered about 20,000 signatures, 36,000 short of the number required.<ref>4</ref>

In the mid-1970s residents of the U.P. and northern Wisconsin worked together on the idea again, citing tax transfers to the south and environmental rules they said were written for downstate. Bumper stickers proposed calling the new state "North Michigan".<ref>5</ref> State Representative [[Dominic Jacobetti]] of [[Negaunee, Michigan|Negaunee]], who chaired the House Appropriations Committee for many years, was among the legislators who introduced statehood legislation in 1975; it did not advance.<ref>6</ref> After the 1970s the idea survived mostly as a joke, on T-shirts and in the song lyrics of local bands.{{citation needed}}

### Before the crisis

By late 2008 the peninsula was already in an economic slump. In December 2008 [[Cliffs Natural Resources]] said it would cut production at the [[Empire Mine|Empire]] and [[Tilden Mine|Tilden]] mines near [[Ishpeming, Michigan|Ishpeming]] from more than 12 million tons of iron ore pellets in 2008 to about 6.7 million tons in 2009, laying off up to 350 of the roughly 1,600 workers at the two mines.<ref>7</ref> Unemployment in several U.P. counties was above 12% in January 2009.{{citation needed}}

### FEMA Northern Tier Emergency District

After the Presidents' Day Massacre of February 16, 2009, and the declaration of a national emergency the next day, FEMA designated the land along the Canadian border from [[Minnesota]] to [[Maine]] as a "Northern Tier Emergency District", divided into sectors under the existing FEMA regions.<ref>9</ref> [[FEMA Region V]] ran the Upper Peninsula sector from the former [[K. I. Sawyer Air Force Base]] in [[Marquette County, Michigan|Marquette County]], a [[Strategic Air Command]] base that closed in 1995 and whose runway had been used since 1999 by [[Sawyer International Airport]]. Federal staff set up in the old base hangars and the empty base housing south of the airport.<ref>10</ref>

The district imposed an 8 p.m. curfew, rationed gasoline and diesel at a limited number of stations, and set up medicine distribution at hospitals in Marquette, [[Escanaba, Michigan|Escanaba]], [[Hancock, Michigan|Hancock]] and Sault Ste. Marie. According to the *Daily Press* of Escanaba, ration cards were issued by county clerks using driver's license addresses, and people with Lower Peninsula licenses were told to "return to their county of residence".<ref>11</ref> Most [[Michigan State Police]] troopers and [[Michigan National Guard]] units in the peninsula were ordered south to [[Detroit]] and Lansing in the last week of February.

On or around March 12 telephone and data links between Sawyer and the Region V office in [[Chicago]] and the Michigan State Police in Lansing became intermittent, and by March 16 supply convoys coming north on [[Interstate 75 in Michigan|I-75]] were stopping at [[Mackinaw City, Michigan|Mackinaw City]], south of the bridge.<ref>12</ref> FEMA staff left Sawyer in the last week of March. In a written notice dated March 25, the sector coordinator handed "custody of remaining federal property" at the site, including fuel tanks, generators and two warehouses of food and medical supplies, to the Marquette County Sheriff's Office.<ref>13</ref>

### Proclamation of statehood

[[Image:sup_decl.jpg|Delegates read the statehood declaration in a courtroom of the Marquette County Courthouse, March 27, 2009.]]

On Friday, March 27, 2009, delegates from the county boards of all fifteen counties met in the main courtroom of the [[Marquette County Courthouse]]. Keweenaw County's two delegates arrived in the afternoon after the road south of [[Houghton, Michigan|Houghton]] was plowed.<ref>14</ref> The meeting adopted a "Declaration of the Counties of the Upper Peninsula", which invoked the 1858 Ontonagon convention, proclaimed the State of Superior and stated that the counties "remain members of the United States of America under its Constitution, and will so remain when lawful government is restored".<ref>14</ref> Former Marquette County Board chairman Arvid J. Lahtinen was chosen as provisional governor by a show of hands. The delegates also declared all former FEMA stocks to be property of the new state.

Speaking on the courthouse steps after the vote, Lahtinen said:

>> Nobody here asked for this. Lansing can't reach us, Washington doesn't exist, and the trucks stopped at the bridge. Somebody has to keep the furnaces lit until spring.<ref>14</ref>

[[Reuters]] and the [[Associated Press]] carried short reports the following day; *[[The Globe and Mail]]* ran a front-page photograph of the courthouse.<ref>15</ref>

### Mackinac Bridge checkpoint

[[Image:sup_bridge.jpg|The State of Superior border checkpoint at the Mackinac Bridge toll plaza in St. Ignace, March 2009.]]

On the evening of March 27 the [[Mackinac Bridge Authority]] toll plaza at [[St. Ignace, Michigan|St. Ignace]] became a checkpoint, staffed by [[Mackinac County, Michigan|Mackinac County]] sheriff's deputies and volunteers. The bridge, which opened on November 1, 1957, carries I-75 across the [[Straits of Mackinac]] and is about 5 miles (26,372 ft; 8,038 m) long.<ref>16</ref> Bridge Authority employees on the north side continued to work, but toll collection was suspended. Southbound traffic was allowed through at first; northbound traffic was limited to residents with U.P. addresses on their licenses, commercial fuel and food shipments, and medical cases.

By mid-April the Lower Peninsula side at Mackinaw City was itself controlled by [[Cheboygan County, Michigan|Cheboygan County]] and [[Emmet County, Michigan|Emmet County]] authorities, and the two sides agreed to close the bridge to most traffic except on Tuesdays and Fridays.{{citation needed}} Ferry service between St. Ignace, [[Mackinac Island]] and Mackinaw City resumed in May, with passengers screened on the St. Ignace dock.

Notable incidents at or near the checkpoint:

* **April 3** – A line of more than 200 vehicles formed on the south approach after rumors that the bridge would close permanently. Most were turned back.<ref>17</ref>
* **April 19** – A man from [[Gaylord, Michigan|Gaylord]] drove through the barrier at the toll plaza and was stopped by deputies on US 2 west of St. Ignace. No shots were fired.
* **June 6** – The checkpoint closed for about 40 hours after reports, later found to be false, that armed groups from Detroit were moving north on I-75.{{citation needed}}
* **August 12** – After news of the nuclear detonations in California, Arizona and Tennessee, about 1,100 people crossed northbound in two days, the largest movement since March. Most were relatives of U.P. residents.<ref>18</ref>

### Soo Locks agreement

The [[Soo Locks]] at Sault Ste. Marie, operated by the [[United States Army Corps of Engineers]] Detroit District, are the only link for large ships between Lake Superior and the lower [[Great Lakes]]. Only the 1,200-foot [[Poe Lock]], opened in 1968, can take the largest "thousand-footer" lake freighters. The locks normally open for the season on March 25 after the winter closure, and were opened on schedule in 2009 under the last instructions received from the District office.<ref>19</ref>

In April the provisional government reached an agreement with the remaining Corps staff at the locks. Under the agreement the locks stay in operation with their existing crews, Superior pays the crews' wages from county funds until federal pay resumes, and part of the lockage fees charged to Canadian and other foreign vessels is paid in fuel delivered to the state.<ref>20</ref> The [[United States Coast Guard]] station at Sault Ste. Marie was not part of the agreement; its cutters remain at their moorings and, according to the *Soo Evening News*, its personnel "have not taken a position".<ref>21</ref> Through the summer most traffic in the locks was Canadian [[grain]] and [[iron ore]] traffic between [[Thunder Bay, Ontario|Thunder Bay]] and ports on [[Lake Erie]] and the [[St. Lawrence Seaway]].

### Summer and autumn 2009

Fuel was the main problem of the summer. The state set up a Fuel Board in May that bought gasoline and diesel in [[Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario]] and resold it through county road commissions.<ref>22</ref> The Convention passed a "Wood Heat Order" in September that allowed residents to cut dead and down timber for firewood on state forest land in the [[Lake Superior State Forest]] and parts of the [[Escanaba River State Forest]].

After the [[FCC Emergency Directive 09-4A]] in September, most U.S. television signals on cable systems in the peninsula went dark. [[WLUC-TV]] in Negaunee and [[WNMU]] at [[Northern Michigan University]] continued to broadcast local news and Convention sessions.{{citation needed}} The first snow fell on the Keweenaw in mid-October.

## Geography

Superior covers the whole Upper Peninsula, about 16,377 square miles (42,420 km²) of land, 29% of Michigan's land area.<ref>8</ref> The [[Montreal River (Wisconsin–Michigan)|Montreal River]], a land boundary through the forest and the [[Menominee River]] separate it from Wisconsin; Lake Superior lies to the north, Ontario across the [[St. Marys River (Michigan–Ontario)|St. Marys River]] to the east, and the [[Lower Peninsula of Michigan|Lower Peninsula]] across the Straits of Mackinac to the south. [[Lake Michigan]] and [[Lake Huron]] form much of the southern shore.

The western part is higher and rockier, including the [[Porcupine Mountains]], the [[Huron Mountains]] and the [[Keweenaw Peninsula]]; [[Mount Arvon]] in [[Baraga County, Michigan|Baraga County]] is the highest point at 1,979 ft (603 m). The eastern part is flatter, with large swamps, the [[Seney National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Tahquamenon Falls State Park]]. The [[Hiawatha National Forest|Hiawatha]] and [[Ottawa National Forest|Ottawa]] national forests, [[Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore]] and [[Isle Royale National Park]] were federal land; their status is unclear since the [[National Park Service]] staff left in spring.{{clarify}}

Winters are long. Lake-effect snow on the Keweenaw regularly passes 200 inches (5 m) a year, and the record winter of 1978–79 brought about 390 inches (9.9 m) at Delaware in [[Keweenaw County, Michigan|Keweenaw County]].{{citation needed}}

Main roads are [[U.S. Route 2 in Michigan|US 2]], [[U.S. Route 41 in Michigan|US 41]], [[M-28 (Michigan highway)|M-28]] and the short northern section of I-75 from St. Ignace to Sault Ste. Marie.

## Government and politics

The Superior Convention has two delegates from each county, chosen by the county boards, for 30 delegates in total. It meets in the Marquette County Courthouse, usually on the first and third Monday of each month; in winter it has agreed to meet by radio link when roads are closed.{{citation needed}} Decisions need a majority of delegates and a majority of counties, a rule that the smaller counties insisted on in March.

The provisional governor has no fixed term. Lahtinen has said he will serve "until there is an election or until somebody better shows up". The Convention discussed holding an election in November 2009 but postponed it in October, citing fuel for polling places and the lack of a voter roll; county clerks still hold the 2008 Michigan registration lists.<ref>23</ref>

Counties keep their existing boards, and policing is in the hands of county sheriffs. About 60 former Michigan State Police troopers who stayed in the peninsula were sworn in as "state deputies" in June.{{citation needed}} Courts continue to sit under Michigan law as it stood on February 16, 2009, unless the Convention changes it.

The five federally recognized tribes in the peninsula (the [[Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians]], the [[Bay Mills Indian Community]], the [[Keweenaw Bay Indian Community]], the [[Lac Vieux Desert Band of Lake Superior Chippewa Indians|Lac Vieux Desert Band]] and the [[Hannahville Indian Community]]) are not members of the Convention. The Sault Tribe sent an observer to the March 27 meeting, and the Convention recognized tribal police authority on reservation land in a resolution in May.<ref>24</ref>

Engineers from [[Michigan Technological University]] in Houghton and Northern Michigan University in Marquette help keep the power grid and communications running. The coal-fired [[Presque Isle Power Plant]] in Marquette, owned by [[We Energies]], is the main source of electricity; its coal arrives by ship, and in August the Convention gave coal shipments priority at the Soo Locks.{{citation needed}}

## Economy

The economy rests on timber, iron mining and tourism. The Empire and Tilden mines on the [[Marquette Iron Range]] went idle in spring 2009. Their pellets had been carried by the [[Lake Superior and Ishpeming Railroad]] to the [[Presque Isle ore dock]] in Marquette and shipped south to steel mills on the lower lakes; when those ports were cut off, there was nowhere to ship them. Some miners were rehired by the counties in the summer to cut firewood and maintain roads.<ref>22</ref>

Most fuel and food now comes in from Ontario over the International Bridge, about 2.8 miles long, which crosses the St. Marys River between the two Sault Ste. Maries. Trucks carry gasoline, diesel, flour and propane north and return with lumber, scrap metal and pulpwood.{{citation needed}} The [[Canadian dollar]] circulates informally near the border, and several banks in Sault Ste. Marie, Michigan accept it at a posted rate.

Tourism, which had depended on visitors from Lower Michigan, [[Chicago]], [[Milwaukee]] and Wisconsin, mostly stopped in 2009. The [[Kewadin Casinos]], owned by the Sault Tribe, stayed open at reduced hours, with many customers coming from Ontario.

## Culture

The western counties have a large population of [[Finnish American]] descent, and they are the only counties in the United States where a plurality of residents report Finnish ancestry. [[Finlandia University]] in Hancock was founded in 1896 as Suomi College. [[Sauna]]s are common, and the [[pasty]], brought by Cornish miners, is the regional food. There are also large groups of [[French Canadian]], [[Swedish American|Swedish]], [[Italian American|Italian]], Cornish and [[Ojibwe]] descent.

Several observers have noted that Finnish-American cooperative traditions, such as the cooperative stores and the [[Työmies]] newspaper in the early twentieth century, were cited by delegates when the counties set up shared fuel and food depots.{{who}} The provisional governor is himself of Finnish descent. At the Juhannus midsummer festival in [[Toivola, Michigan|Toivola]] in June, the Superior flag was flown alongside the [[Flag of Finland|Finnish flag]].

## Flag

The flag is based on the "Yooper flag" popular in the Upper Peninsula in the 2000s: a dark green field with a white border and a white outline of the peninsula in the center. In April 2009 the Convention dropped the original slogans such as "YOU BETCHA!" and replaced them with the word "SUPERIOR".<ref>25</ref> A proposal to add a five-pointed star for the 1858 convention was rejected.

## Relations

### Michigan

The Michigan state government in Lansing has not recognized the Convention. In a statement read on a Lansing radio station in April, a spokesman for the state government called the proclamation "an emergency arrangement by county officials, not a change in the boundaries of Michigan".<ref>17</ref> Superior's position is that it is not seceding from Michigan but "holding the counties in trust". No Michigan official has visited the peninsula since March. Communication with Detroit has been out since mid-March.

### Wisconsin

The three road crossings to Wisconsin, at [[Menominee, Michigan|Menominee]]–[[Marinette, Wisconsin|Marinette]] on US 41, at Iron Mountain on US 2 and [[U.S. Route 141|US 141]], and at Ironwood–[[Hurley, Wisconsin|Hurley]] on US 2, stayed open throughout 2009. Menominee and Marinette counties share a hospital district and a bridge, and their sheriffs signed a local agreement in April.<ref>26</ref> Some officials in northern Wisconsin counties have attended Convention sessions as observers, and *[[The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel]]* reported in July that a petition in [[Vilas County, Wisconsin|Vilas]] and [[Iron County, Wisconsin|Iron]] counties asked to join Superior, as some of the 1858 and 1970s proposals had planned.<ref>27</ref> The Convention has not acted on it.

### Canada

The [[Canada Border Services Agency]] port of entry at [[Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario]] (population about 75,000) has stayed open, but since April only commercial trucks, residents with Canadian documents and approved medical cases have been allowed in. Canada has accepted about 2,400 refugee claims at the Sault crossing in 2009.<ref>28</ref> The federal government in [[Ottawa]] has not recognized Superior and deals with it through the City of Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, and the [[Government of Ontario|Ontario government]] on "practical matters of the border".<ref>29</ref> The Ontario government has not commented in public on the Soo Locks agreement, though Canadian vessels use the locks.

### Federal government

The [[Federal Government of the United States (2009–present)|federal government]] in [[Sacramento, California|Sacramento]] has not recognized Superior's statehood. In July it said that "existing state boundaries remain in force" in all areas outside federal control. Superior sent a letter to the [[Interim Congress]] in June asking for recognition as a "provisional territory"; no reply has been reported.{{citation needed}}
