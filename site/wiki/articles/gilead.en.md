# Republic of Gilead

> {{Current}} This article documents a **current event**. Information may change rapidly as the event progresses, and initial news reports may be unreliable.
> {{POV}} The neutrality of this article is disputed. Please see the discussion on the talk page. (October 2009)
> {{About}} This article is about the regime proclaimed in Washington in 2009. For the biblical region, see [[Gilead]]. For other uses, see [[Gilead (disambiguation)]].

The **Republic of Gilead**, officially the **Divine Republic of Gilead**, is a regime proclaimed in [[Washington, D.C.]] in April 2009 by the "[[Sons of Jacob]]", a military-religious movement that seized the federal capital after the [[Presidents' Day Massacre]]. As of November 2009 Gilead controls the [[Interstate 95]] urban corridor of the [[Northeastern United States]], from [[Northern Virginia]] through [[Baltimore]], [[Philadelphia]] and [[New York City]] to metropolitan [[Boston]], and most of the rest of [[New England]], together with three districts in northern [[Maine]], [[New Hampshire]] and northeastern [[Vermont]] which it governs separately.

Gilead is not recognized by any member state of the [[United Nations]]. The [[United States Government (2009–)|U.S. federal government]] in [[Sacramento]], [[Canada]] and the [[European Union]] regard it as an illegal occupying authority. The [[Holy See]] recalled its nuncio from Washington after Gilead declared the [[Catholic Church]] a "hostile sect". Because Gilead does not admit foreign journalists and has cut international telephone lines, most of what is known about it comes from refugee testimony, documents smuggled out to Canada, and monitored broadcasts, and many details below are disputed.

## Name

"Gilead" is taken from the region of [[Gilead]] east of the [[Jordan River]] in the [[Hebrew Bible]]; Jeremiah 8:22 asks "Is there no balm in Gilead; is there no physician there?".<ref>8</ref> The name first appears in mimeographed pamphlets circulated among groups linked to the Sons of Jacob in 2006–2007.<ref>1</ref> The regime's own broadcasts use "the Republic of Gilead", "Gilead" and, in edicts, "the Divine Republic". Canadian government documents use "the Gilead authorities" or "the de facto authorities in Washington". Refugees interviewed in [[New Brunswick]] said that the words "Under His Eye" are used as an everyday greeting and farewell in Gilead-held towns.<ref>7</ref>

## History

### Background

Between 2003 and 2008 birth rates fell steadily in the United States and most industrialized countries. From 2006 the [[Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] used the term "Birth Dearth" in its reports.<ref>2</ref> Outbreaks of a drug-resistant strain of [[syphilis]] (dubbed "R-strain" by the press) and scares over chemical contamination and nuclear plant accidents added to public unease.

"Sons of Jacob" is a collective name for a network of conservative think tanks and churches that took shape around 2005, advocating a "restoration of the traditional family order" as the answer to the fertility crisis. Its members included retired officers, private security contractors and a number of state legislators, but it had little influence in national politics before 2009.<ref>3</ref> According to *The Atlantic*, the network held closed meetings at rented conference centres in [[Northern Virginia]] and [[Maryland]] from 2006, at which position papers on "fertile women as a national resource" were circulated. Several participants later said they took the papers to be a thought exercise.<ref>3</ref>{{citation needed}}

### Presidents' Day Massacre

{{Main|Presidents' Day Massacre}}

On 16 February 2009 ([[Presidents' Day]]) the White House was bombed, and gunmen opened fire on a joint session of [[United States Congress|Congress]] convened to hear the President's address on a "national fertility emergency". The President, the Vice President and most members present were killed. The attack was initially blamed on "Islamic fanatics", a claim that no independent inquiry has confirmed.<ref>4</ref> The designated survivor, Attorney General Raymond E. Calloway, was sworn in as Acting President that night at an undisclosed location, and moved the federal government to Sacramento in early March.

On 17 February the military in Washington declared a national emergency and "temporarily suspended" the [[United States Constitution|Constitution]], imposing a curfew and press censorship and introducing the Identipass. The BBC reported in May that several officers who signed the 17 February declaration were later identified as members of Sons of Jacob study groups, and that the speed with which checkpoints went up around the [[Capital Beltway]] suggested advance planning.<ref>4</ref>

### Compucard freeze

In March 2009 the provisional authorities froze all bank accounts and Compucards held by women, transferring the funds to husbands or male next of kin, and ordered women dismissed from employment. The Associated Press reported that the order took effect overnight on 9–10 March, and that women in Washington and Baltimore found their cards refused at shops the next morning; accounts were reportedly identified by a sex code already held in the Compucard database.<ref>11</ref> The Sacramento government declared the directive void on 23 March, but it continued to be applied wherever the Washington authorities controlled the banks.

Protest marches in [[Boston]] and Washington were fired upon.<ref>5</ref> In Boston, troops opened fire on marchers on [[Boylston Street]] on 20 March. The number of dead has never been established; figures given by witnesses to the *Boston Globe* ranged from "a dozen" to more than forty.<ref>12</ref> A march toward the [[United States Capitol|Capitol]] in Washington the following weekend was dispersed with tear gas and live rounds near [[Union Station (Washington, D.C.)|Union Station]].

### Proclamation and "the War"

In April 2009 the provisional government proclaimed the Republic of Gilead in Washington and named the [[Religious Society of Friends|Quakers]], [[Catholic Church|Catholics]] and [[Baptists]] "hostile sects". Most sources give 7 April as the date of the proclamation, read on what had been [[WTOP]] radio; some refugee accounts put it a day earlier.{{citation needed}} Through the spring and summer Gilead fought on two fronts, which its broadcasts together call "the War".

### Appalachian front

Gilead's forces in the west are styled the 4th Division, "Angels of the Apocalypse" (apparently unrelated to the U.S. Army's [[4th Infantry Division (United States)|4th Infantry Division]] at [[Fort Hood]]). From late April they fought Baptist militias in the [[Appalachian Mountains]], first along the [[Blue Ridge Mountains|Blue Ridge]] passes west of [[Loudoun County, Virginia|Loudoun County]]: [[Snickers Gap]] on [[Virginia State Route 7|Route 7]], and the [[Interstate 66]] approach to [[Front Royal, Virginia|Front Royal]].<ref>16</ref> Gilead radio repeatedly announced that the Angels were "smoking out" pockets of Baptist guerrillas in the "Appalachian Highlands" with air support. The guerrillas are now usually identified with the [[Appalachian Guerrilla Government]] in the [[Shenandoah Valley]] and the [[West Virginia Baptist Convention]] further west. By August the front had settled roughly along the Loudoun–[[Clarke County, Virginia|Clarke]] county line and the Blue Ridge crest, and [[Skyline Drive]] in [[Shenandoah National Park]] was reported closed and mined. Casualty figures from both sides cannot be checked.

Gilead broadcasts also claimed that a "21st Battalion, Angels of Light" was fighting in [[Detroit]] in May. Street fighting was reported there, but Detroit is now within the [[Radio Silence zone]] and it is not clear that any Gilead unit was involved; most analysts treat the claim as propaganda.{{citation needed}}

### I-95 advance

The second column advanced north along I-95. The order of events, mostly from wire reports, was:

* **Late April:** Baltimore secured; the [[Fort McHenry Tunnel]] closed to civilian traffic. [[Fort George G. Meade|Fort Meade]] and [[Aberdeen Proving Ground]] were reported to have come over without fighting.{{citation needed}}
* **2 May:** Gilead troops at the [[Delaware Memorial Bridge]]; [[Wilmington, Delaware|Wilmington]] taken shortly afterwards.
* **9–16 May:** Fighting in and around Philadelphia. Reuters reported armoured vehicles on [[Interstate 95 in Pennsylvania|I-95]] near the [[Philadelphia International Airport|airport]] on 11 May and gunfire in [[Kensington, Philadelphia|Kensington]] for several days.<ref>13</ref> The [[Pennsylvania National Guard]] units in the city withdrew west toward [[Harrisburg]].
* **Late May:** Advance up the [[New Jersey Turnpike]]; [[Trenton, New Jersey|Trenton]] and [[New Brunswick, New Jersey|New Brunswick]] occupied. Large numbers of residents fled west on [[Interstate 78|I-78]] and [[Interstate 80|I-80]].
* **29 May – 21 June:** Battle for New York City. Gilead units crossed from [[Staten Island]] and from [[Fort Lee, New Jersey|Fort Lee]], where the [[George Washington Bridge]] was closed after an explosion on its upper level on 4 June. Elements of the [[42nd Infantry Division (United States)|42nd Infantry Division]] of the [[New York Army National Guard]] held parts of the [[the Bronx|Bronx]] until mid-June before pulling back north.<ref>14</ref> Manhattan was declared "pacified" on 21 June. One estimate is that more than 1.5 million people left the city during the fighting.{{citation needed}}
* **Early July:** [[Long Island]] occupied through [[Nassau County, New York|Nassau]] and [[Suffolk County, New York|Suffolk]] counties. Gilead troops moved along I-95 through [[Fairfield County, Connecticut|Fairfield County]] and [[New Haven, Connecticut|New Haven]]; [[Naval Submarine Base New London]] at [[Groton, Connecticut|Groton]] was evacuated by sea before they arrived.{{citation needed}}
* **14 July:** [[Providence, Rhode Island|Providence]] occupied.
* **18–26 July:** Boston. Fighting was reported along [[Massachusetts Route 128|Route 128]] and in [[Dorchester, Boston|Dorchester]]; [[Hanscom Air Force Base]] fell on about 22 July. After the city was taken, several ministries were moved into the buildings of [[Harvard University]] in Cambridge.
* **Early August:** The advance south of Boston stopped on the [[Norfolk County, Massachusetts|Norfolk]]–[[Bristol County, Massachusetts|Bristol]] county line around [[Foxborough, Massachusetts|Foxborough]] and [[Mansfield, Massachusetts|Mansfield]], where it met the [[Blackwater Security Concession]].

### Northern Maine and the Northeast Kingdom

Gilead's hold on northern New England came mostly without battles. In August church councils in [[Bangor, Maine|Bangor]] and [[Presque Isle, Maine|Presque Isle]] declared for "the provisional authority in Washington", and on 14 August the *Bangor Daily News* reported that a convoy had reached Bangor on I-95 the previous day.<ref>17</ref> The Aroostook March and the Penobscot Covenant District were set up over the following weeks. Gilead troops reached [[Houlton, Maine|Houlton]], where I-95 ends at the Canadian border, in late August.<ref>28</ref> In the [[Northeast Kingdom]] of Vermont, Gilead took [[St. Johnsbury, Vermont|St. Johnsbury]] in early September, and with it the [[Interstate 91]] crossing at [[Derby Line, Vermont|Derby Line]].

### Resettlement of the Children of Ham

{{Main|Resettlement of the Children of Ham}}

In July 2009 Gilead announced the "Resettlement of the Children of Ham" to "National Homeland One" in [[North Dakota]]. The first announcement monitored by the [[BBC Monitoring]] service was broadcast on 22 July and said that resettlement was "proceeding on schedule" and that "three thousand a day" were being moved.<ref>29</ref> The [[American Civil Liberties Union|ACLU]] and several international human rights groups have called it a forced deportation of African Americans, and on 4 August the [[United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights|UN High Commissioner for Human Rights]] called on Gilead to stop it.<ref>6</ref>

Refugees have described roundups in Baltimore, [[Prince George's County, Maryland|Prince George's County]], Philadelphia and [[Newark, New Jersey|Newark]], and a holding camp at [[Fort Dix]] in [[Burlington County, New Jersey|Burlington County]]. Where the trains go is not known. North Dakota is not under Gilead control, officials in North Dakota have said no such trains have arrived, and the Sacramento government says "National Homeland One" does not exist.{{citation needed}} Some human rights groups fear the deportees are being held in camps inside Gilead or have been killed; Gilead has not allowed the [[International Committee of the Red Cross]] to visit.

### Jewish repatriation boats

Gilead also began a "repatriation" scheme for [[Jews]], who were reportedly given a choice between conversion and emigration to [[Israel]]. Ships chartered by Gilead left [[Port Newark–Elizabeth Marine Terminal|Port Newark]] and Baltimore from July. Gilead radio claimed in October that 19,000 people had departed. Israel's [[Ministry of Immigrant Absorption]] said on 20 October that it had registered about 6,800 arrivals at [[Haifa]] from Gilead ports, which would leave a large number unaccounted for.<ref>20</ref> One ship, the Panamanian-flagged *Atlantic Dawn*, with about 400 passengers, stopped answering radio calls west of the [[Azores]] in September and has not been found.<ref>21</ref> Some arrivals in Haifa said other passengers had been put into boats at sea; this has not been verified.

### Relations with northeastern neighbours

Southeastern [[Massachusetts]] ([[Plymouth County, Massachusetts|Plymouth County]] to [[Nantucket]]) and the [[Rhode Island]] counties of [[Bristol County, Rhode Island|Bristol]] and [[Newport County, Rhode Island|Newport]] are held by the [[Blackwater Security Concession]], which faces Gilead along the line south of I-95. The line has changed hands several times; in September both sides claimed the [[Attleboro, Massachusetts|Attleboro]] rail yard. There is no known agreement between them, though trucks have been reported crossing at [[Massachusetts Route 24|Route 24]].{{citation needed}}

The [[Republic of Vermont]] and the [[Green Mountain Republic]] reject Gilead's claim to Vermont's three northeastern counties. The Republic of Vermont closed [[U.S. Route 2 in Vermont|U.S. Route 2]] east of [[Danville, Vermont|Danville]] in September and says it is sheltering several thousand people from the Kingdom in [[Montpelier, Vermont|Montpelier]] and [[Barre, Vermont|Barre]].

## Geography

{{See also|Interstate 95|Northeast Corridor}}

Gilead's territory falls into two very different parts. The southern core is the densely built [[Northeast megalopolis]] along I-95 and the [[Northeast Corridor]] railway, from the [[Potomac River]] to the Boston basin, with the [[Chesapeake Bay|Chesapeake]] and [[Delaware Bay|Delaware]] bays and the [[Hudson River|Hudson]] estuary. Gilead also claims the [[Delmarva Peninsula]] counties of Delaware and the [[Eastern Shore of Virginia]] ([[Accomack County, Virginia|Accomack]] and [[Northampton County, Virginia|Northampton]]), most of Maryland east of [[Frederick County, Maryland|Frederick County]], all of [[New Jersey]], [[Connecticut]] and New Hampshire, and Maine south of the Penobscot district. Control of rural western Massachusetts and of interior Maine is said to be thin, limited to the main roads and the larger towns; estimates of the area under effective control (c. 124,500 km²) are about two thirds of the area claimed.{{citation needed}}

The northern part is three thinly populated districts, administered separately from Washington and described in Gilead broadcasts as "Marches" or "Covenant Districts".

### Northern districts

**Aroostook March** ([[Aroostook County, Maine|Aroostook County]], seat [[Presque Isle, Maine|Presque Isle]]) is the largest county east of the [[Mississippi River]] and borders New Brunswick and Quebec on two sides. Its economy rests on potatoes: about 90 percent of Maine's crop is grown here, and Maine farmers harvested an estimated 54,500 acres in 2008.<ref>25</ref> The former [[Loring Air Force Base]] at [[Limestone, Maine|Limestone]], closed in 1994, is reported to be in use as a Gilead depot.{{citation needed}} The border crossings at Houlton–[[Woodstock, New Brunswick|Woodstock]], [[Madawaska, Maine|Madawaska]]–[[Edmundston]] and [[Fort Kent, Maine|Fort Kent]]–[[Clair, New Brunswick|Clair]] were the main refugee routes into New Brunswick until Gilead closed them in September.

**Penobscot Covenant District** ([[Penobscot County, Maine|Penobscot]] and [[Piscataquis County, Maine|Piscataquis]] counties, seat Bangor, pop. 31,473 in 2000) covers the Penobscot basin and the timberlands around [[Moosehead Lake]]. Its paper mills at [[Millinocket, Maine|Millinocket]] and [[East Millinocket, Maine|East Millinocket]] had largely shut before the crisis. [[Bangor International Airport]] is reported to be in military use.

**District of the Northeast Kingdom** ([[Orleans County, Vermont|Orleans]], [[Caledonia County, Vermont|Caledonia]] and [[Essex County, Vermont|Essex]] counties in Vermont and [[Coös County, New Hampshire|Coös County]] in New Hampshire, seat St. Johnsbury) lies on the upper [[Connecticut River]] north of the [[White Mountains (New Hampshire)|White Mountains]]. It is an exclave in the sense that the only road link to the rest of Gilead is [[U.S. Route 3|U.S. 3]] and [[Interstate 93|I-93]] through [[Franconia Notch]].

### Military installations

Bases reported under Gilead control include [[Andrews Air Force Base]], [[Fort Belvoir]], [[Marine Corps Base Quantico]], Fort Meade, Aberdeen Proving Ground, the [[United States Naval Academy]] at [[Annapolis, Maryland|Annapolis]], [[McGuire Air Force Base]] and Fort Dix, Hanscom Air Force Base and [[Portsmouth Naval Shipyard]] at [[Kittery, Maine|Kittery]]. The status of [[Naval Air Station Brunswick]] is unclear.{{citation needed}}

## Politics


Gilead has no written constitution. Power rests with the Council of the Sons of Jacob, which governs by edict. B. Frederick Judd is described in broadcasts as Chairman of the Provisional Supreme Council; he has been seen in public only a handful of times, and no biography has been published.<ref>3</ref> Frederick R. Waterford is said by *The Globe and Mail*'s sources to have been responsible for the regime's dress rules and public ceremonies, and to have worked in market research before 2009.<ref>7</ref>{{who}} Joseph Lawrence, reportedly a former university economist, is credited in leaked papers with the economic plan and with the labour camps known as the "Colonies".<ref>7</ref>

According to leaked internal documents, society is ranked by male titles ("Commander", "Guardian", "Angel") and assigned female roles.<ref>7</ref> Refugee accounts collected by Canadian agencies describe the following, though the terms and their meanings vary between accounts:

* **Commanders** of the Faithful: senior officials, allotted large houses in Washington, [[Chevy Chase, Maryland|Chevy Chase]] and Cambridge.
* **Angels**: soldiers at the fronts. **Guardians** of the Faith: police and household guards.
* **Eyes**: the secret police. Refugees said they used black vans.
* **Wives**: wives of Commanders. **Econowives**: wives of lower-ranking men.
* **Handmaids**: women who had borne children, assigned to Commanders' households to bear children for them. This is the most disputed point in the leaked papers; Gilead's broadcasts have never used the word.
* **Marthas**: domestic servants. **Aunts**: women who train and supervise the Handmaids, reportedly at a centre in a former school gymnasium.
* **Unwomen**: women who refuse an assigned role, and who are said to be sent to the Colonies.

Public executions, which refugees call "Salvagings", have been reported in Boston, and bodies are said to be displayed on the wall around [[Harvard Yard]].{{citation needed}} Because of press controls, outside knowledge of conditions in Gilead comes mainly from refugee testimony and interviews by Canadian refugee agencies.

### Religion

The April proclamation named Quakers, Catholics and Baptists as hostile sects. Catholic churches in Philadelphia and Boston were reported closed in May and June, and priests arrested. The position of Baptists is confused: Gilead fights Baptist guerrillas in Appalachia, but its northern Maine districts were set up with the help of local evangelical and independent Baptist churches. Several refugees told CBC News that membership in "approved congregations" decided who received ration cards in Aroostook County.<ref>15</ref>

## Underground Femaleroad and Quaker smuggling

Refugees and Canadian aid workers describe an escape network that refugees call the "Underground Femaleroad", after the [[Underground Railroad]] of the 19th century, in which Quakers in Philadelphia had also played a large part.<ref>24</ref> Quaker meetings in Pennsylvania, New Jersey and New England are said to have hidden women and moved them north in stages, using farm trucks and logging roads. Gilead's broadcasts call it the work of "Quaker traitors"; at least eleven people were hanged in Philadelphia in June for "smuggling of women", according to the BBC.<ref>4</ref>

The main routes reported are:

* Through the woods of western Maine to [[Jackman, Maine|Jackman]] and across the border to [[Armstrong, Quebec|Armstrong]] on [[U.S. Route 201]].
* Through the Northeast Kingdom to [[Stanstead, Quebec|Stanstead]] and the [[Eastern Townships]], before Derby Line was taken.
* By small boat from the [[Down East]] coast of [[Washington County, Maine|Washington County]] to [[Campobello Island]] and [[Grand Manan]], and on to [[Yarmouth, Nova Scotia|Yarmouth]]. Gilead closed the [[Franklin Delano Roosevelt Bridge|FDR International Bridge]] between [[Lubec, Maine|Lubec]] and Campobello in August.

The [[Canadian Friends Service Committee]] in [[Toronto]] has said it is in contact with the network but has given no details. Some refugees also mention "Mayday" as a name for an armed resistance group in Gilead; nothing is known about it.{{citation needed}}

## Refugees

{{Main|Gilead refugee crisis in Canada}}

Canada closed its border crossings with Gilead-held territory but opened refugee channels. According to [[Citizenship and Immigration Canada]], 212,400 people who had left Gilead-held territory were registered in Canada by 31 October 2009.<ref>18</ref> The [[United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees|UNHCR]] said in October that the true number could be "closer to 300,000", since many crossed without registering or reached Ontario through upstate [[New York (state)|New York]], which Gilead does not control.<ref>19</ref> About two thirds of adult arrivals are women.

The tent camp at CFB Gagetown passed 30,000 residents in early October, according to the *Telegraph-Journal*.<ref>23</ref> *La Presse* reported in September that Valcartier was being winterized.<ref>22</ref> In Toronto, the *Toronto Star* has described an area of rooming houses and church halls taken over by arrivals, which residents have started to call "Little America".<ref>30</ref>

## Economy

{{Expand section}}

Little reliable information is available. Container traffic at Port Newark–Elizabeth and the [[Port of Baltimore]] is reported to have mostly stopped, and the Northeast Corridor railway is used for military traffic. Women may not hold property or money; all wages are paid into men's Compucard accounts. Rationing of fuel and food was reported in New York and Philadelphia from July.{{citation needed}} Refugees say the Colonies are camps where women and men classed as Unwomen or "Gender Traitors" clean up contaminated industrial sites.

## Flag and symbols

The flag first appeared in footage from Washington in May 2009. It keeps the thirteen red and white stripes of the [[Flag of the United States|U.S. flag]], but the canton carries a ring of white stars around an [[Eye of Providence]] on a blue field. The motto "Under His Eye" appears on the seal used on edicts.
