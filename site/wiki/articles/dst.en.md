# Deseret

**Deseret** is a [[U.S. state|state]] in the [[Western United States]], on the western edge of the [[Colorado Plateau]] where it meets the [[Great Basin]]. It is bordered by [[Utah]] to the north, west and south and by [[Colorado]] to the east. It has a land area of about 11,170 square miles. Its population was 56,409 at the [[United States Census, 2000|2000 census]], the smallest of any state. The capital is [[Manti, Deseret|Manti]] and the largest city is [[Richfield, Deseret|Richfield]].

The name comes from the [[Book of Mormon]], where it means "honeybee". [[Latter Day Saint]] settlers organized the provisional [[State of Deseret (1849)|State of Deseret]] in 1849. On January 4, 1896, the central counties around the Sanpete and Sevier valleys were admitted to the Union as the State of Deseret, on the same day as Utah.<ref>1</ref>

Since February 2009 the state government has said it is maintaining constitutional order. It does not recognize the [[Republic of Gilead]], and its contact with the [[Federal Government of the United States (2009–present)|federal government in Sacramento]] has largely been cut off.

## History

### Provisional state and Utah Territory

In March 1849 Latter Day Saints in the [[Salt Lake Valley]] held a convention, organized the provisional State of Deseret, elected [[Brigham Young]] governor and petitioned Congress for statehood. In November of that year [[Isaac Morley]] led about two hundred settlers into the Sanpete Valley and founded the settlement that became Manti.

Congress did not accept the provisional state. Under the [[Compromise of 1850]] it created [[Utah Territory]] instead. The provisional legislature kept meeting for another twenty years and was known as the "ghost government". The central settlements went through the [[Walker War]] (1853–1854) and the [[Black Hawk War (1865–1872)|Black Hawk War]] (1865–1872), much of which was fought in Sanpete and Sevier counties.

### Partition and statehood

In the 1880s Congress put pressure on the territory over [[polygamy]]. The [[Manti Temple]] was dedicated in May 1888. After the [[Dakota Territory]] was divided and admitted as two states in 1889, members of Congress proposed dividing Utah Territory in the same way.<ref>2</ref> The Enabling Act of July 16, 1894 formed five counties south of [[Utah Valley]], on both sides of the [[Wasatch Plateau]], into a state that kept the 1849 name of Deseret; the rest of the territory became Utah. Both states were admitted on January 4, 1896.{{citation needed}}

Critics have long argued that a state of a few tens of thousands of people should not hold two seats in the [[United States Senate|Senate]].<ref>3</ref>

### 20th century

A school founded at [[Ephraim, Deseret|Ephraim]] in 1888 later became [[Snow College]]. Turkey farming in Sanpete County became the state's main industry from the 1930s. In the 1970s the [[Huntington Power Plant|Huntington]] and [[Hunter Power Plant|Hunter]] coal-fired plants were built in [[Emery County, Deseret|Emery County]] to supply the cities of the [[Wasatch Front]] to the north. [[Capitol Reef National Park]], established in 1971, is in [[Wayne County, Deseret|Wayne County]].

## 2009 crisis

On February 17 Governor Christensen declared a state of emergency and ordered the [[Deseret National Guard]] to set up checkpoints on [[U.S. Route 89]] north of Fairview and on [[Interstate 70]].<ref>4</ref>

On April 14 the Legislature adopted the Manti Resolution, which says the state constitution remains in force and that the state government will exercise all powers itself until a "lawfully elected federal government" is restored.<ref>5</ref> In June the state reached an agreement with the [[LDS Church]] administration in [[Salt Lake City]] to trade electricity from the Huntington and Hunter plants for fuel and medical supplies.<ref>6</ref>

The *[[Sanpete Messenger]]* reported that by September about 4,800 evacuees from Utah Valley and [[Nevada]] had registered in the state, about 600 of them housed on the Snow College campus.<ref>7</ref> After the August detonation at [[Kingman, Arizona|Kingman]], the state health department set up radiation monitoring at Richfield and [[Green River, Deseret|Green River]]. After the November 4 detonation at [[Millington, Tennessee|Millington]], state officials said Deseret was outside any fallout area.<ref>8</ref>

## Geography

The west of the state is the valley of the [[Sevier River]] and the Sanpete Valley, at about 5,200 to 6,200 feet. The center is the [[Wasatch Plateau]] and the [[Fish Lake Plateau]]. The east is the desert of the [[San Rafael Swell]] and the [[Green River (Colorado River tributary)|Green River]]. The highest point, Delano Peak, is in the [[Tushar Mountains]] in the south-west.

## Economy

{{Expand section}}

Agriculture is mainly turkeys, alfalfa and dairy. The coal mines and power plants of Emery County are the largest industrial employers.{{citation needed}} Capitol Reef National Park, [[Goblin Valley State Park]] and [[Fish Lake (Utah)|Fish Lake]] are the main tourist destinations.
