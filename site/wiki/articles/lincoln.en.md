# State of Lincoln

> 【模板:现正发生】This article documents a **current event**. Information may change rapidly as the event progresses, and initial news reports may be unreliable.
> 【模板:消歧义】This article is about the self-governing state formed in 2009. For the historical statehood proposals, see [[Lincoln (proposed Northwestern state)]]. For the Texas proposal, see [[Lincoln (proposed Southern state)]].

The **State of Lincoln** is a de facto self-governing state formed after the [[Presidents' Day Massacre]] by counties of [[Eastern Washington]], the [[Idaho Panhandle]] and north-central and northeastern [[Oregon]]. Its capital is [[Spokane, Washington|Spokane]]. The region, known as the [[Inland Northwest]] or "Inland Empire", has sought separate statehood since 1865, and the name honors [[Abraham Lincoln]]. On February 20, 2009, the Spokane County Commission and the Spokane City Council met in joint session and proclaimed a "Provisional Government of the State of Lincoln".

Lincoln professes loyalty to the Constitution of the United States but does not recognize the authority of the state governments in [[Olympia, Washington|Olympia]], [[Boise, Idaho|Boise]] or [[Salem, Oregon|Salem]]. Its western border runs roughly along the eastern slope of the [[Cascade Range]], where it meets [[Cascadia]]; the two dispute control of [[Grand Coulee Dam]]. As of November 2009 thirty-six counties take part in the provisional legislature. The [[Federal Government of the United States (2009–present)|federal government]] in [[Sacramento, California|Sacramento]] had not recognized its statehood.

## History

### Early proposals

{{Main|Lincoln (proposed Northwestern state)}}

When [[Idaho Territory]] was organized in 1863, its capital was [[Lewiston, Idaho|Lewiston]]. After the capital moved to [[Boise, Idaho|Boise]] in the winter of 1864–65, residents of the north asked for a separate state of "Lincoln", without success. In the later 1860s a Lewiston-centered movement sought a "Columbia Territory" combining eastern Washington, northern Idaho and western Montana.<ref>1</ref>

A 1901 proposal would have joined the Idaho Panhandle to Eastern Washington as the state of Lincoln. In April 1907 Spokane boosters went further, calling for a state made of eastern Washington, northeastern Oregon and northern Idaho, with the western boundary moved to 120° W to line up with California's eastern border.<ref>2</ref> In January 1915 a Spokane state senator introduced a joint memorial in the [[Washington State Legislature]] asking Congress to create Lincoln, which drew wide discussion in the eastern counties.<ref>3</ref> Similar bills in 1996, 1999 and 2005 never left committee.{{citation needed}}

### 2009 provisional government

[[Image:lin_2009.jpg|County Commissioner Gordon E. Hoaglund proclaims the provisional government outside the Spokane County Courthouse, February 20, 2009.]]

After the Presidents' Day Massacre of February 16, 2009, the Washington state government declared an emergency and moved most of the [[Washington National Guard]] to [[Seattle]] and the port of [[Tacoma, Washington|Tacoma]]. Communications between the eastern counties and Olympia were intermittent for days, and [[Interstate 90]] over [[Snoqualmie Pass]] was closed for a time by Guard checkpoints.<ref>9</ref>

On Friday, February 20, the Spokane County Commission and the Spokane City Council met jointly at the [[Spokane County Courthouse]]. Pointing to the 1907 Spokane proposal and what Hoaglund called "a hundred and forty years of asking politely", they proclaimed a provisional government of the State of Lincoln, with County Commissioner Gordon E. Hoaglund as provisional governor.<ref>4</ref> According to the *Spokesman-Review*, the vote was 7–1 on the city council and unanimous on the commission. The resolution was read from the courthouse steps at about 4:30 p.m. Hoaglund told reporters that "nobody here is leaving the United States. We are leaving a phone line that nobody in Olympia is answering."<ref>4</ref>

[[Kootenai County, Idaho|Kootenai]], [[Bonner County, Idaho|Bonner]], [[Boundary County, Idaho|Boundary]] and other North Idaho counties joined on February 28; the [[Tri-Cities, Washington|Tri-Cities]], [[Yakima County, Washington|Yakima County]] and Oregon's [[Umatilla County, Oregon|Umatilla County]] followed in March.<ref>10</ref><ref>12</ref> The joining resolutions were mostly passed by county commissions on their own authority. In Latah and Whitman counties, which hold the [[University of Idaho]] and [[Washington State University]], the votes were contested by faculty groups and student organizations, who argued that a county commission had no power to change the state it belonged to.<ref>11</ref>

On April 14 a provisional legislature made up of former state legislators from eastern districts adopted the Spokane Charter in Spokane. It provides for a governor elected by the legislature, serving "until constitutional order is restored in the United States".<ref>5</ref> The legislature confirmed Hoaglund as governor the same afternoon, 54 votes to 4, with three seats vacant.<ref>5</ref>

### Hanford and the Columbia

{{Main|Hanford Site}}

Lincoln's territory includes the 586-square-mile (1,518 km²) [[Hanford Site]] in [[Benton County, Washington|Benton County]], the operating [[Columbia Generating Station]] (1,170 MWe net), and the chemical weapons stockpile at [[Umatilla Chemical Depot]] near [[Hermiston, Oregon|Hermiston]], which held about 12 percent of the original U.S. stockpile.<ref>21</ref> By February 2009 the sarin and VX munitions at Umatilla had already been destroyed; the mustard agent ton containers were still in storage when the depot's incinerator was shut down on February 18 "pending clarification of the chain of command".<ref>13</ref>

In March 2009 the provisional government reached an arrangement with the [[United States Department of Energy|Department of Energy]]'s Richland office: contractor staff stayed on, and the Lincoln State Guard took over perimeter security.<ref>6</ref> Under the arrangement the DOE's Richland Operations Office and the [[Office of River Protection]] continue to run the tank farms and the unfinished [[Hanford Waste Treatment Plant|Waste Treatment Plant]], with payroll met from contractor reserves and, since June, from a Lincoln "emergency services fund" raised by Benton and Franklin counties. A DOE spokesman in Sacramento said in May that the department "has not transferred any authority over any federal site to any entity".<ref>17</ref> Workers at the [[Hanford Reach]] said shifts had been cut by about a third.{{citation needed}}

Lincoln controls [[McNary Dam]], [[John Day Dam]], [[The Dalles Dam]] and the four lower [[Snake River]] dams ([[Ice Harbor Dam|Ice Harbor]], [[Lower Monumental Dam|Lower Monumental]], [[Little Goose Dam|Little Goose]] and [[Lower Granite Dam|Lower Granite]]), as well as [[Priest Rapids Dam|Priest Rapids]] and [[Wanapum Dam|Wanapum]] on the mid-Columbia, owned by the [[Grant County Public Utility District|Grant County PUD]]. Grand Coulee upstream is held by Cascadia, as are [[Chief Joseph Dam|Chief Joseph]], [[Wells Dam|Wells]], [[Rocky Reach Dam|Rocky Reach]] and [[Rock Island Dam|Rock Island]] in Chelan and Douglas counties, which did not join Lincoln. [[Bonneville Dam]], at the western end of the gorge, is also in Cascadian hands.

### Bonneville Power dispute

The two states clashed over dispatch on the [[Bonneville Power Administration]] (BPA) grid. The BPA's main control center at Vancouver, Washington is on the Cascadian side; the backup Munro Control Center near Spokane was taken over by Lincoln on March 3, when about forty BPA dispatchers there agreed to stay at their posts.<ref>15</ref> For most of March and April the two centers issued conflicting dispatch orders, and on April 9 a fault on the [[Grand Coulee]]–Spokane lines caused a blackout in parts of Spokane and Stevens counties lasting about eleven hours.<ref>15</ref>

On April 22 Cascadian officials cut transfers from Grand Coulee to Lincoln for two days, citing "unpaid balances". Lincoln responded by drawing down reservoirs on the [[Spokane River]] {{citation needed}} and by threatening to stop the Columbia Generating Station's output to the western grid. Negotiations at [[Ellensburg, Washington|Ellensburg]] produced an interim protocol on August 6, 2009: since then a joint dispatch group of engineers from both states has run the grid from Vancouver and Munro on alternate weeks, with power exchanges settled monthly in dollars.<ref>16</ref> The protocol covers Grand Coulee only "as a generating unit"; the question of who owns the dam is left open.<ref>16</ref>

### Okanogan and the Grand Coulee standoff

Okanogan County was the last Washington county to join. Its commissioners voted 2–1 on June 2, after Cascadian Guard units at Grand Coulee closed [[Washington State Route 155|SR 155]] across the dam to Lincoln vehicles for three weeks in May.<ref>23</ref> The town of [[Coulee Dam, Washington|Coulee Dam]], which straddles Okanogan, Grant and Douglas counties, is effectively divided, with the dam itself, the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] offices and the Douglas County side held by Cascadia.<ref>14</ref> The [[Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation]] have declined to recognize either government and run their own checkpoints on reservation roads.{{citation needed}}

## Geography

Lincoln stretches from the crest of the [[Bitterroot Range]] (the Idaho–Montana line) west to the eastern slope of the Cascades, and from [[British Columbia]] south to the southern foot of the [[Blue Mountains (Pacific Northwest)|Blue Mountains]], where it meets the [[State of Jefferson]]. It includes the [[Columbia Basin]], the wheat hills of the [[Palouse]], the [[Yakima Valley]] and the lower [[Snake River]] valley as far up as Lewiston.

The western boundary with Cascadia follows county lines rather than the crest itself. Chelan and Douglas counties, around [[Wenatchee, Washington|Wenatchee]], stayed with Olympia, so the border bends east there to the Columbia River. South of the river, Lincoln holds Wasco County and The Dalles, and the line with Cascadia crosses the [[Columbia River Gorge]] at the Hood River–Wasco county line near [[Mosier, Oregon|Mosier]].

## Government and politics

The provisional legislature has 61 seats, held by former state legislators from districts in Eastern Washington, North Idaho and northeastern Oregon. According to the Charter, 33 seats belong to the eleven Washington legislative districts east of the Cascades, 21 to Idaho's seven northern districts and 7 to the Oregon districts; seats whose holders refused to serve are filled by appointment of the county commissions concerned.<ref>5</ref> The legislature sits in the chambers of the Spokane City Council.

Counties keep their existing commissions. Policing is in the hands of county [[Sheriffs in the United States|sheriffs]] and the Lincoln State Guard, formed in March 2009 from Washington and Idaho Guard members who had stayed in the east, retired service members and volunteers. Its commander is Colonel (ret.) Everett L. Sondergaard. The Guard's strength was given as "about 3,100" in September.<ref>19</ref>

The Charter keeps Washington, Idaho and Oregon law in force within each county "as it stood on February 15, 2009", so Idaho's and Oregon's different tax and liquor laws still apply on their own side of the old state lines.<ref>5</ref> A Lincoln Court of Appeals, made up of three retired superior court judges, sat for the first time in Spokane on July 13.{{citation needed}}

### Elections

{{Expand section}}

The Charter calls for legislative elections "within one year after communications with the national government are restored". No date has been set. Some legislators from Yakima and Kittitas counties have called for an election in spring 2010 regardless.

## Checkpoints and transport

Lincoln's main road links run along [[Interstate 90]], [[Interstate 82]] and [[Interstate 84]], with [[U.S. Route 395]] and [[U.S. Route 195]] serving north–south traffic. Since March the State Guard has kept fixed checkpoints at the main crossings. Travelers must show a driver's license or a county-issued "resident card"; Identipasses issued under the federal emergency are not accepted.

### Snoqualmie Pass

The I-90 crossing of [[Snoqualmie Pass]] (3,015 ft) is Lincoln's main link with [[Seattle]], about 280 miles from Spokane. The Washington Guard checkpoint on the summit, set up on February 17, was withdrawn on March 2.<ref>9</ref> The State Guard has since kept a checkpoint at the [[Easton, Washington|Easton]] interchange in Kittitas County, about 15 miles east of the summit, and Cascadian police keep one at [[North Bend, Washington|North Bend]]. Commercial trucks are inspected for weapons and fuel. Traffic was stopped completely from April 22 to April 24 during the Grand Coulee power cut and again for one night after the August nuclear detonations in California.<ref>16</ref> Winter closures for avalanche control are expected to cause further disruption.

### Lookout Pass

At the eastern end, the State Guard keeps a checkpoint on I-90 at [[Lookout Pass]] (4,710 ft) on the Idaho–Montana line. Traffic from [[Montana]] is light. Most of it is freight bound for Lewiston and Spokane rail yards and families from western Montana seeking supplies. Lincoln officials say contact with authorities on the Montana side "comes and goes".<ref>19</ref>

### Other crossings

* [[U.S. Route 12]] over [[White Pass (Washington)|White Pass]] (State Guard post at Rimrock; closed in heavy snow)
* [[U.S. Route 97]] over [[Blewett Pass]], on the Kittitas–Chelan line
* [[Interstate 84]] at Mosier, Oregon, on the Columbia River Gorge border
* [[Interstate 84]] east of La Grande toward Baker City
* Canadian border crossings at [[Oroville, Washington|Oroville]]–[[Osoyoos]], [[Laurier, Washington|Laurier]], [[Porthill, Idaho|Porthill]] and [[Eastport, Idaho|Eastport]], which the [[Canada Border Services Agency]] has kept open for Canadian citizens and commercial goods<ref>20</ref>

## Military installations

[[Fairchild Air Force Base]], 12 miles west of Spokane, is home to the [[92d Air Refueling Wing]] and the Washington Air National Guard's [[141st Air Refueling Wing]], flying the [[KC-135 Stratotanker]]. The garrisons at Fairchild and the [[Yakima Training Center]] have declared for neither side and keep in contact with the State Guard.<ref>6</ref> Fairchild's commander said in a June statement that the base "remains under federal orders" and would not "take sides in a dispute among Americans". Flying has been limited since April by fuel supply, and the base buys jet fuel through the Spokane terminal of a pipeline that runs from Montana refineries.<ref>22</ref> {{citation needed}}

The [[Air Force Survival School]] at Fairchild suspended classes in March. Some tanker crews have reportedly been flown to Sacramento.{{who}}

## Economy

The economy rests on [[Palouse]] wheat, [[Yakima Valley]] apples and [[hops]], irrigated farming in the Columbia Basin, and timber. Before the crisis the Yakima Valley grew roughly three-quarters of the U.S. hop crop, and Whitman County was often listed as the largest wheat-producing county in the country.{{citation needed}}

### Wheat and the Snake River barges

Most Inland Northwest wheat, mainly soft white, is grown for export to Asia. Before 2009 it moved by barge from Lewiston, 465 river miles from the Pacific, through the eight dams of the Columbia–Snake system to export elevators at Portland, Vancouver and Longview.<ref>20</ref> These terminals and the Bonneville lock are in Cascadia. On May 4 the lock was closed to barges coming from Lincoln for twelve days, in what the *Oregonian* called "a dispute over tolls nobody had heard of before".<ref>18</ref>

With the western ports cut off, grain exports now move by barge up the [[Snake River]] to Lewiston and then east by rail and road. Tri-City elevators at [[Pasco, Washington|Pasco]] and [[Kennewick, Washington|Kennewick]] load barges for the trip upriver through Ice Harbor and Lower Granite, a reversal of the normal flow. From Lewiston, grain goes by truck over [[U.S. Route 95]] and by the [[Camas Prairie Railroad|Camas Prairie]] line to the main lines at Spokane.<ref>24</ref> Elevator operators told the *Capital Press* in July that only about a third of the 2009 harvest had a buyer.<ref>24</ref> The harvest itself was reported as average.

### Energy

Lincoln sells surplus power from the lower Columbia and Snake dams and the Columbia Generating Station to Cascadia under the August protocol. Gasoline has been rationed in Spokane, Yakima and the Tri-Cities since March; the ration was raised to 10 gallons a week per registered vehicle in October.{{citation needed}}

## Refugees

{{See also|Cascadia}}

Lincoln has taken in people from the Seattle side since late February. Most came over Snoqualmie Pass after the March unrest in Seattle and Tacoma, and a second group came in August, after the nuclear detonations at Concord–Mare Island and other sites in California, when people arrived from as far as the Bay Area by way of Oregon. The provisional government opened reception centers at the Kittitas Valley Event Center in Ellensburg and the Grant County Fairgrounds in [[Moses Lake, Washington|Moses Lake]].<ref>14</ref>

Figures differ. The Lincoln Office of Emergency Management gave "more than 41,000" registered arrivals by October 1; the Spokane [[Catholic Charities]] office and church groups put the total, including those staying with relatives, at between 60,000 and 90,000.<ref>25</ref> Housing is short in Ellensburg, [[Moses Lake, Washington|Moses Lake]] and the Tri-Cities. In September Kittitas County commissioners asked the State Guard to limit the number of vehicles let through at Easton, and since October 5 arrivals without a host family or a job offer have been sent on to camps in Grant County.<ref>25</ref>

Some refugees are women who left Seattle and Portland to avoid the Compucard freeze being applied by employers and banks there. Spokane banks have said they "do not apply" the March directive, but at least two Yakima credit unions did apply it until a legislative resolution in June.<ref>26</ref>

## Relations

### Cascadia

Relations with Cascadia are the most important and the most strained. Apart from the Grand Coulee and BPA questions, the two governments disagree over the [[Washington State Patrol]], whose eastern district offices passed to Lincoln, and over state pension and payroll funds held in Olympia. Officials from both sides have met at Ellensburg, at the [[Central Washington University]] campus, roughly every two weeks since June.<ref>16</ref>

### State of Jefferson

Lincoln and Jefferson share a short border along the Blue Mountains in central Oregon, around Grant County. Hoaglund and Jefferson's provisional governor Wade H. Pruett exchanged letters in April recognizing each other's "provisional authority".<ref>27</ref> The two states have agreed to let livestock trucks cross on [[U.S. Route 395]] without inspection.

### Federal government

The federal government of Acting President Raymond E. Calloway in Sacramento has not recognized Lincoln. In a letter of May 19 Hoaglund asked Sacramento to treat Lincoln "as a state in formation, as Congress treated West Virginia in 1863". The White House press office in Sacramento said the letter had been "received".<ref>17</ref> Federal agencies with staff in Lincoln, including the DOE, the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[United States Army Corps of Engineers|Corps of Engineers]] Walla Walla District and the [[United States Postal Service|Postal Service]], deal with Lincoln officials on a day-to-day basis without recognizing them.

### Other reactions

* {{flagicon|Canada}} [[Canada]]: The [[British Columbia]] government said in March that it would "keep the crossings open for humanitarian reasons" and deal with "whatever local authority is in place". The [[Royal Canadian Mounted Police|RCMP]] increased patrols near Osoyoos and [[Creston, British Columbia|Creston]].<ref>20</ref>
* {{flagicon|USA}} State of Idaho: The Idaho government in Boise called the northern counties' votes "of no legal effect" and has not set up any checkpoint on [[U.S. Route 95]] south of Grangeville.
* {{flagicon|USA}} State of Oregon: Governor's office in Salem called the decisions "unlawful but understandable".{{citation needed}}
* {{flagicon|Gilead}} [[Republic of Gilead]]: Radio Gilead described Lincoln and Cascadia in July as "rebellious provinces of the old order". It has no presence in the region.
* Several tribal governments, including the [[Yakama Nation]] and the [[Nez Perce Tribe]], have said that their treaty rights "are not affected" by the formation of Lincoln.<ref>12</ref>

## Flag

The flag was adopted by the provisional government in March 2009: a dark blue field with a white disc in the center bearing a golden sheaf of wheat tied with a blue ribbon over blue waves for the Columbia River, with "LINCOLN" arched above and "1865 · 2009" below, marking the first proposal and the founding of the provisional government.
